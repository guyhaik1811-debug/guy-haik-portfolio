South America · Colombia · Taganga

Taganga, November 2017

November 2017Guy Haik

I was in Taganga twice, a fishing village a few minutes from Santa Marta. The first time was on the way in from Bogotá, on November 23. I came back after Palomino, for November 30 and December 1, and both times I stayed at the same place: Benjamin Hostel. The first stay has photos from one evening. The second had a boat trip to Playa Cristal and an afternoon at another hostel on the hill. I was alone for the second stay.

My conclusion up front: Taganga is a good place to land after a big city, and a good base for the boats. I do not remember how I got there from Bogotá or from Palomino, what I paid for the hostel or the boat, or the hour the boat left, and I did not write any of it down. I have no photos from the 24th or the 29th, so I do not know where I spent those days.

The times are my phone's clock, and Google Photos stores them in Colombian time (GMT-5). The boxes hold research I did in November 2026, each with a source and a date, so you can tell it apart from what I remember.

What to know today, in short (checked November 2026)
  • Boats to Tayrona: they leave Taganga usually between 09:30 and 11:00, about 45 minutes in calm weather and up to two and a half hours in rough weather. Tickets are not sold online. I found no boat that stops at Playa Cristal on a fixed schedule.
  • Costs: a one-way fare of 63,000 to 150,000 pesos, according to one page. The park fee for a foreign visitor in 2026 is 81,000 pesos in low season and 96,500 in high season, from the national parks resolution as reported by Infobae. An older page that quotes 50,000 is out of date.
  • The park closes for a period each year: this year it was closed from October 19 to November 2.
  • Hostels: Benjamin Taganga and Hostel TNT Mirador are still listed online, but I could not confirm they are open or what a bed costs.

The sources and the details are in the boxes below the story. These things change, so check before you go.

November 23: the hostel and the bay

On Thursday, November 23, I slept at Benjamin Hostel in Taganga, a small fishing village a few minutes from Santa Marta. I do not remember how I got there from Bogotá, what I paid, or how many nights I stayed. I only have photos of the bay, taken between 17:07 and 17:26, near sunset: wooden fishing boats pulled up on a dark beach, small boats at anchor, and green hills with white buildings climbing up the slope.

Benjamin Hostel (checked November 2026)

What I found: Hostelworld still lists Benjamin Taganga at Calle 8a A1-43, in front of the sea. The listing describes air-conditioned rooms with private bathrooms and hot water, an on-site diving school and a restaurant, with free Wi-Fi, a 24-hour front desk, a tours desk and luggage storage. Breakfast is not included (Hostelworld).

What I did not find: a bed price, a score for the hostel itself (the page shows only a 6.8 for the town of Taganga, from 80 reviews), and any sign that it is open today. A listing that is still online does not mean the place is open.

November 30: by boat to Playa Cristal

On the 30th I took a boat from Taganga to Playa Cristal, a beach inside Tayrona National Park. I went alone. I do not remember the price, the company, the departure time, or whether I paid the park fee on top. By midday it was clear, and the photos are bright.

The beach, around 12:50

The first photos are from about 12:50. The sand is pale and the water is clear turquoise, with a green headland closing the bay at the far end. At the first spot the water was calm and I took pictures of the bay and a small boat tied off the point. A few steps along there were plastic sun loungers under a tree, and people sitting at the water's edge, swimming and walking by. I blurred the faces of everyone who is close enough to be recognised.

Getting to Playa Cristal by boat (checked November 2026)

What I found: boats to Tayrona leave from Taganga, usually between 09:30 and 11:00. A calm crossing takes about 45 minutes, but rough weather can stretch it to two and a half hours. A one-way fare is 63,000 to 150,000 pesos. Most public boats go straight to Cabo San Juan with no stops, while Playa Cristal is usually reached on a private boat or in a tour. Boat tickets are not sold online. The same page says the park fee for foreigners is about 97,000 pesos in high season and 81,000 in low season, plus 6,000 pesos of insurance a day, paid in cash. High season includes December 15 to January 30. This year the park was closed from October 19 to November 2 and reopened on November 3 (Gecko Routes, updated October 19, 2026).

An undated tour page lists a day trip to Neguanje and Playa Cristal at 170,000 pesos per adult, from 06:30 to 16:00, with a sailing between the two beaches, and says that non-residents pay a park tax of 50,000 pesos on top, a figure that does not match the 2026 fee (81,000 or 96,500 pesos) (Nomades).

What I did not find: the 2017 price. The boat pages disagree on the park fee, but the 2026 national parks rates are 81,000 pesos in low season and 96,500 in high season for a foreign visitor (Infobae), and the 50,000 figure is not current. Rates can change, so check the park's own website before you go. I also did not find a Taganga boat that stops at Playa Cristal on a fixed schedule.

From the shade, around 13:40

At around 13:38 I took a picture from under a thatched roof: a green slope with a tall cactus, and the sea beyond it, with big clouds over the mountains. I do not know which building it was. It looks like a rest area or a restaurant on the hill above the beach. Two minutes later I took a wide view of the bay from the top of the slope, with a pale sky and only a few swimmers in the water.

The ride back, around 17:00

The last photo of the day was at about 17:00, from the boat on the way back. The sun is low and the sea is dark and choppy, with a headland in silhouette and a small tower or mast on its far point. I do not know which boat or which company it was, or whether we left the beach at a set time.

December 1: a hostel on the hill

On the 1st I walked to another hostel, Hostel Mirador, within walking distance of Benjamin. I did not stay there. I went to see the place, and the photos are from that visit, between 15:38 and 16:54. I slept at Benjamin again that night. I am not sure which of the several Taganga places with a similar name it was, but a hill hostel with a pool, a bar and a terrace called Hostel TNT Mirador fits what I saw. I did not confirm it, so treat the name as a guess.

What I remember is the terrace. It looks straight down on the bay, with a steep road below it where a small blue bus climbs a bend, and a building with turquoise curtains next to it. At 15:38 the sky was bright, at 15:53 a speedboat left a long white wake across the bay, and at 16:37 the cloud had thickened.

Two photos at 15:58 and 16:03 show me lying on a big round daybed with white cushions, on a covered terrace with vines on the railing and a cushion printed with an elephant. I do not remember why I took two.

The last four photos are from 16:37 to 16:54, in the late afternoon. In the first, the bay is grey under a thick sky, with the bus still on the bend. In the next three the view is through branches and cables, with a thatched roof among the trees, and in the last the sky is softer and the light on the boats is warmer.

The two hostels (checked November 2026)

What I found: the Benjamin Taganga page lists an address at Calle 8a A1-43, about 0.2 km from the centre, in front of the sea, with 24-hour reception, WiFi and a tours desk. It shows no prices, and the town as a whole is rated 6.8 from 80 reviews (Hostelworld). The page for Hostel TNT Mirador lists an address at Carrera 2B #18-149, about 0.8 km from the centre, on a hill, with a pool, a bar and a restaurant. Its rating is 4.3 from 77 reviews, and some reviewers describe it as isolated on a hill (Hostelworld).

What I did not find: prices for either, since neither page shows them without a date search, and proof that the hostel I visited is TNT Mirador. I also did not find out whether the hostels are in business under the same name today, so check before you book.

What I do not remember

I do not remember how I got from Bogotá to Taganga or from Palomino to Taganga, what I paid for Benjamin Hostel, or what the boat to Playa Cristal cost and who ran it. I also took no notes.
