Ninh Binh and Hanoi, October 17 to 22, 2019: Tam Coc, a scooter day, Cuc Phuong and a last evening by West Lake

October 17 to 22, 2019Guy Haik

On the evening of October 17, 2019 I left Hà Giang on a night bus to Tam Cốc, in Ninh Bình province, south of Hanoi. I stayed in the area until October 21, then took a bus to Hanoi for my last night in Vietnam on this trip. This page is about those five days: a village among limestone hills, a day by scooter, a national park, the bus back and an evening by West Lake.

In short: a night bus from Hà Giang, a bamboo bungalow, a walk to a viewpoint and a cave with colored lights, a scooter day to a temple gate and a sunset, a day at Vân Long and Cúc Phương National Park, a bus to Hanoi, a free walking tour, an egg coffee and a hotel I recommend. I did not keep receipts, so most prices below are prices listed today, not what I paid. Where I do not remember something, I say so.

What to know today, in short (checked November 2026)
  • The night bus from Hà Giang to Tam Cốc: Baolau lists departures at 19:30 and arrival at 04:30, 9 hours, for 400,000 to 550,000 dong depending on the bus. GoAsia lists a standard sleeper at 11 to 15 dollars and a cabin bus at 17 to 24, 7 to 10 hours, and says to check whether the bus stops at Tam Cốc or at the Ninh Bình bus station. I do not remember what I paid or when I arrived.
  • Tam Cốc, the boat and the entrance: Vietnamtourism lists the entry fee at 120,000 dong and the boat at 150,000 dong per boat. Ninh Binh Private Car lists 250,000 dong per adult with the boat included. The sources disagree, so ask at the ticket office. I do not remember taking the boat ride.
  • A scooter for the day: Vietnamtourism gives 100,000 to 150,000 dong a day, and Ninh Binh Private Car lists 80,000 to 100,000 for a manual bike and 100,000 to 150,000 for an automatic. Bikesbooking lists mid-size bikes from 650,000. The price depends on the shop and the bike. Photograph the bike before you leave, and ask about the deposit.
  • Bái Đính Pagoda: entry to the grounds is free. Vietnamtourism lists the electric shuttle at 30,000 dong and the cable car at 60,000, and says it is open from 6am to 6pm. Vinpearl says 7am to 9pm, and lists the motorbike parking at 15,000 dong. The hours differ, so check on arrival.
  • Cúc Phương National Park: Vinpearl lists the adult entrance at 60,000 dong. Vietnamtourism says the gate is about 45 km from Ninh Bình city and the primate rescue center is the first stop, 500 meters past the gate. Hours are listed as 6am to 7pm by Hanoi Eco Tour and 7am to 8pm by Bestpricetravel.
  • From Tam Cốc to Hanoi by bus: Rome2rio gives about 101 km, about 1 hour 20 minutes, hourly, at about 5 to 9 dollars. 12Go lists limousine minibuses at about 6 to 14 dollars. Reviews there mention being dropped far from the stop that was booked, so check the drop-off point before you book.
  • A free walking tour in Hanoi: the tours on GuruWalk are free to book and you pay what you think it was worth, in cash, at the end. FreeTour says most walkers give between 10 dollars and 20 euros per person.
  • An egg coffee in Hanoi: Pickyourtrail lists 45,000 to 60,000 dong a cup, and Pholio, verified April 2026 lists 35,000 to 55,000.
  • Where I slept: for the bungalow in Tam Cốc, Expedia listed it from about 16 dollars a night in December 2025. I did not find a current price. For the hotel in Hanoi I did not find a current listing.

These prices change, so check before you go.

October 17: the night bus

On the 17th I came back to Hà Giang city, returned the motorbike, and took an overnight bus to Tam Cốc. At 19:54 I photographed the inside of the bus, with the rows of bunks, but that photo shows nothing I can use. I do not remember the company, the price, the time we arrived or where it dropped me. My first photos in Tam Cốc are from 16:09 the next day.

Tam Cốc: where I stayed

I think I stayed at Eco Field Nest Tam Coc, in a bamboo bungalow near the rice fields, and I recommend it. I did not keep the booking, so I am going by memory. On the morning of October 21, at 10:13, I filmed a 45-second tour of the room: a room with walls lined with bamboo, a sloping ceiling of bamboo and wood, two single beds with white sheets, a painting of a rice field on the wall, an air conditioner and a bathroom with a sink. Outside there is a paved path with scooters parked along it, a wooden pergola with orange pillars and limestone hills behind. I do not remember where I slept on the first nights, or whether it was the same place. Photos in Google Photos put me near the Tam Cốc and Bích Động area, though the location is only an estimate.

October 18: a village, a viewpoint and a cave

From about 16:09 to 16:30 I walked through a village behind the rice fields. I photographed a goat standing on a rocky bank among the bushes, a few ducks in the yard of a house with a stone pillar and a pond with small huts on the shore. I do not know the name of the village.

At 17:06 to 17:10 I reached a viewpoint over a green valley of limestone peaks, with a river winding through it and the sun low behind the hills. I do not remember the name of the viewpoint, how long the climb took or whether there was a fee. From the same trip I have a photo of a gorge with a still lake and the first moon in the sky.

At 17:35 to 17:43, on the flat ground by the water, I photographed a water buffalo grazing on the bank, and wooden boats waiting at a landing. Tourists were in some of the boats, so I do not show those photos. I do not remember whether this was near the Tam Cốc boat station.

At 18:13 to 18:19 I entered a cave with a floating bamboo walkway. The walkway runs along the water inside the cave, and colored lights, red, green and blue, light the rock. Near the walkway there is a white statue of a mermaid, and I took a selfie next to it, which I do not publish. I do not remember the name of the cave or what I paid. It is the part of the day I liked best, because the walkway and the light make it look like another world.

October 19: a scooter to Bái Đính

I rented a scooter from the hostel I was staying at. I do not remember the price or the name of the shop. I asked another traveler if they wanted to join me, and they said yes, so we spent the day together. I do not publish photos of them, or a name.

My recommendation for the day: do not plan too much. It is a place to be surrounded by nature, and I would not skip anything. At 14:23 I photographed rocks on a hill, with my companion, which I do not publish, and at 15:43 I filmed a line of ox carts moving along a road in the fields. It is not a tour for tourists. I think they were farmers at work.

The route: I asked Google Maps for the road from Tam Cốc to Bái Đính, and it gives about 24 km and about 35 minutes by car. I do not remember which road we took or how long it took us by scooter. We stopped at a lake at sunset at 17:15, and at 17:21 to 17:23 we reached the gate of Bái Đính Pagoda. I did not go in. I think it was closed. A big gate with a curved tiled roof stood in front of golden carved doors, with Chinese characters on the top.

Since I did not go in, I cannot say what the complex is like. The sources I found say the grounds are free and large, and the cable car and the shuttle are extra. If the hours matter to you, read the box at the top: the sources disagree about when it closes.

October 20: Vân Long and Cúc Phương

On October 20 I went by scooter, again, to Vân Long, a wetland nature reserve north of Tam Cốc, and then to Cúc Phương National Park. Google Photos puts me near Vân Long. I did not take a boat there, and I do not remember how much I spent. At 10:56 I filmed a 46-second video from the road: a channel with rows of small wooden boats tied up along the bank, flat fields of reeds, a few tiny boats far out on the water and steep limestone hills behind. At 11:13 I filmed a short video of a resort I passed, with traditional curved roofs, a lily pond and a fountain. I did not go in, and I do not review it.

Cúc Phương is Vietnam's oldest national park, and I got to its gate by scooter. Inside, I walked to a lake with a swing on its bank, a tall ancient tree with a blue sign, and a cave that, as the sign says, belonged to prehistoric people. It did not take long, and I recommend it.

There are also centers for rescued animals in the park: a primate rescue center and a turtle center. I saw them, and I do not recommend going to see animals kept in cages as an attraction, whether you are a local or a tourist. If you go to the park, skip the centers and go for the forest, the ancient tree and the cave. A woman in a cap at the turtle center is an employee of the place. I do not publish photos of the cages or of staff.

At the end of the day I stopped by a wide reservoir near the exit. I do not know its name. I also have a photo of a map of the park from a sign, with handwritten numbers on it, and I do not know what the numbers mean.

October 21: the bus to Hanoi

On the morning of the 21st I filmed the bungalow, and then I took a bus to Hanoi. I do not remember the company, the departure time, the price, or where it dropped me. By 15:52 I was already in Hanoi, in the Tây Hồ area by West Lake. At that time I took a screenshot of Google Maps showing an 11-minute walk along Xuân Diệu street, toward a hostel I did not stay at. I do not remember why I looked it up, and I do not publish its name.

West Lake and my last night

From 16:54 to 17:09 I walked along West Lake and photographed it at sunset: the skyline of tall towers across the water, houses along the bank, and a long white building standing on stilts in the water with the sun behind it. I do not remember whether this was before or after the walking tour.

I slept at a hotel called ShinChin, and I recommend it. I did not find a current listing online, so check that it still exists before you book. I filmed the room at 00:50 on October 22, 45 seconds: a spacious room with a large dark wardrobe, armchairs with white cushions, a mirror, wide windows with blue curtains, wooden bunk beds with white sheets, an air conditioner and a private bathroom with a glass shower and towels. I do not remember whether I had the room to myself, or what I paid.

After I arrived I joined a free walking tour with a local guide, booked through GuruWalk. These tours are free to book and you pay at the end what you think it was worth. I recommend it, and it is customary to tip the guide, although in principle it is free. I do not remember the day or the hour, the meeting point, how long it lasted, or the name of the guide.

At 20:26 I photographed an egg coffee (cà phê trứng), a Hanoi specialty: strong coffee with a thick cream made of egg yolk, sugar and condensed milk, served in a white cup, here with a chocolate pattern on top. Google Photos puts it in the Old Quarter, near Hoàn Kiếm Lake, but only as an area. I do not remember the name of the place, what I paid or whether someone recommended it. The drink was invented in Hanoi in 1946, according to Pickyourtrail, which names Café Giảng as the place where it began. I do not know whether that was my café.

What I recommend

If you have these days, this is what I would do
  • Tam Cốc: stay in a bungalow among the rice fields, and walk in the village in the late afternoon. Go up to a viewpoint for the sunset and go into the cave with the floating walkway if you find it.
  • A scooter day: do not plan too much. Be in the landscape, and go slowly. If you want Bái Đính, check the hours first, because I arrived at the gate and did not go in.
  • Cúc Phương: the forest, the ancient tree and the cave, which took little time. Skip the centers where animals are in cages.
  • Hanoi: a free walking tour with a local guide, a tip at the end, a walk along West Lake at sunset and an egg coffee.

That is my own view, from five days in 2019.
