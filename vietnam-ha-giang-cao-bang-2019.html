Ha Giang, Cao Bang and Ba Be, October 10 to 17, 2019: a scooter in the far north of Vietnam

October 10 to 17, 2019Guy Haik

From October 10 to 17, 2019 I traveled the far north of Vietnam by scooter: from Hà Giang up to the flag tower at Lũng Cú, then east along the border to Cao Bằng, the Bản Giốc waterfall and Ba Bể lake, and back to Hà Giang. This page follows those eight days in order.

In short: a bus to Hà Giang, a ceremony I walked into by chance, a rented scooter, two days of mountain road to the northern border, a night in Cao Bằng, a waterfall and a cave on the border, a lake with moored boats, and a long ride back. I did not keep receipts, so the prices below are prices listed today, not what I paid. Where I do not remember something, I say so. I leave out the hostel's name, every photo with other people in it, a screenshot of a private message, and license plates.

What to know today, in short (checked November 2026)
  • A scooter in Hà Giang: a rental site lists a Honda Future 125 automatic at about 12 euros a day for 3 days, on Riderly, and a Yamaha Sirius 110 at about 7 euros a day, also on Riderly. A guest wrote in May 2025 that he got a 125cc automatic for 3 days for 800,000 dong. I rented for about 4 to 5 days, and I do not remember the price or the shop, so these are today's prices, not mine.
  • A border-area permit: Time Out reports that some tour operators say foreigners entering certain border areas in Hà Giang may need a permit from June 1, 2026, with an extra fee of around 10 dollars. It says most travelers arrange it through the tour company, hostel or homestay. I had no such permit in 2019 that I remember, so ask your hostel.
  • The Lũng Cú flag tower: Vietnam Airlines' guide lists the entrance at 25,000 dong per adult, free for children under 1.3 meters. Jungle Boss adds about 15,000 dong if you take the little electric car to the flagpole, and says the climb is 389 steps. A visitor on Wanderlog wrote that he paid 40,000, so check at the gate.
  • A night in Cao Bằng: Hostelworld describes Cao Bang Eco Homestay as run by an owner who speaks English well, with free bicycles, tours of the Cao Bằng loop and motorbikes for rent. I found no current price for that exact place. Booking's homestay list for Cao Bằng shows an average of about 18 dollars a night, and Hostelz lists dorm beds at about 4 to 5 dollars a night on average.
  • Bản Giốc waterfall: Local Vietnam lists 45,000 dong entrance, cash only, open daily 07:30 to 17:00, and a bamboo raft at 50,000 for about 10 minutes. Jungle Boss says the raft is 50,000 to 100,000 and a child between 1 and 1.3 meters pays 20,000. Vietnam Airlines lists motorcycle parking at 10,000. I did not take the boat.
  • Ngườm Ngao cave: North Vietnam lists the entrance at about 45,000 dong, and Show Caves lists it open daily 07:30 to 17:00. Holidify lists 45,000 for the old route and 195,000 for the new route and old route together, so the price depends on the route. I do not know which route I took.
  • Ba Bể lake: I found no reliable current price for a boat or for entering the park. One site lists about 5 dollars for the park and I could not confirm it. I did not take a boat.
  • Petrol: Viet Nam News reports that from 3pm on November 5, 2026 the maximum retail price was 20,898 dong a liter for E5 RON 92 and 21,156 for E10 RON 95-III. Prices are adjusted often, and a small scooter takes only a few liters a day. I do not remember what I paid.
  • How far it is: Jungle Boss puts Bản Giốc about 85 km and 2 hours by car from Cao Bằng city.

These prices change, so check before you go.

October 10 and 11: Hà Giang

On Thursday, October 10, I moved from the Sapa area to Hà Giang, the city in the far north from which people ride the Hà Giang loop. Google Photos' pins put my first photo of the day at the Lao Cai railway station and the rest of the day in Hà Giang city. I have no photo or video from 05:53 to 19:48, so I do not know how I got from Lao Cai to Hà Giang, or what it cost. Listings I found today show a bus or limousine van of about 6 hours.

In the evening I walked into a big square in the city. At 19:47 I filmed a walk across the square at night, with motorbikes, stalls and lights, and at the end a lit stage with screens. I came by chance and I paid nothing. The stage was a ceremony. The sign says 'Lễ tôn vinh và trao danh hiệu', a ceremony to honor and award titles, and I read the rest as outstanding farmers of Hà Giang, 2019. A part of the word is hidden behind lights, so that reading is mine. I photographed dancers in blue costumes with wooden buckets and baskets. At 20:15 I filmed a big screen in the square with a broadcast of a street festival, with lit floats and a crowd. I do not show the audience or the videos.

At 21:11 I photographed an egg coffee in a café with red chairs: a small clay cup with a foam of whisked egg, on a clay warmer with a tea light under it. I do not give the café's name. I think egg coffee is an acquired taste, and at first it is not tasty. I do not remember what I paid.

After midnight, at 01:00 on October 11, I photographed a printed map of the Hà Giang loop in my hand. I do not show it, because the hostel's name and phone numbers are printed on it. It shows a three-day route: day 1, Hà Giang, Quan Ba, Yên Minh, Đồng Văn; day 2, Đồng Văn, Lũng Cú, Mã Pí Lèng, Mèo Vạc, Du Già; day 3, Du Già, Tam Sơn, Hà Giang. I marked two stretches with a highlighter and wrote notes in pen, including a word I read as 'Bumpground' next to road 34. I slept in a hostel in Hà Giang city and I do not give its name.

On October 11 I mostly walked around the city. My photos are three: one at 13:57 and two at 16:57 and 16:58, of the river that runs through the city, taken from a bridge. Google Photos pins them in Hà Giang city, near a hospital. I do not know where I slept that night, and the photos cannot tell me, so I do not give it.

October 12 and 13: the road to the northern border

I organized the ride through a post in a Facebook group, and I joined a group of travelers from it. On October 12 I left Hà Giang on a rented scooter that I rode myself. I do not remember where I rented it. I left my luggage in storage at the hostel, which I think is the hostel I slept in. I do not remember the price of the scooter, the shop or the time I left. Google Maps cannot show a departure time for a scooter, so I give what my photos show: portraits at 11:08 and 11:15, which I do not show (the 11:15 one shows a handwritten note of three numbers, 1,650,000, 1,200,000 and 2,150,000 dong, one crossed out; I think it has to do with the train, and I am not sure), and the first photo of the road at 12:31. Google Photos pins that one near Thần Luông, on road QL4C, in what Google now labels Tuyên Quang province. From 14:02 to 16:14 I took 23 landscape photos. I did not pay for entrance or parking anywhere that day. We were a group at first, and then we split. I met travelers on the road who joined spontaneously, and I continued with them, and that was more fun. On this page the photos of the road are from October 12 or 13, and I do not know which is which.

On October 13 I stood in front of the flag tower at Lũng Cú, the northernmost point of Vietnam. My photos of the flag are from about 15:14. Many other tourists were there, so I show only the flag. I think I did not pay for the entrance or for parking, and I think I climbed the stairs of the tower. I am not sure of either, so check the price in the box above.

Between 15:34 and 15:39 I stopped at a roadside scene in a village: two children on a scooter that had run out of petrol. A traveler I had met at a gas station, who joined us, was there, and we siphoned petrol from his motorbike into the children's scooter. I do not show the children or the traveler, and I do not write what I know about him. At 15:45 I took a selfie with a dog, which I do not show. I do not remember where the village was. I do not know where I slept on October 12 or 13, and I do not give a name I cannot confirm.

October 14: along a river to Cao Bằng

October 14 was a long day on a winding road along a river, with rice terraces, fields and empty roads. I took many photos from the saddle and from viewpoints, from 10:17 in the morning to 16:04. I do not remember the route or the distance. Nobody decided to leave the Hà Giang loop and go to Cao Bằng. We just flowed. The traveler from the gas station was a perfect partner for the road, and it felt meant to be. At 16:14 I filmed a crossing that was flooded. I do not describe the video. I found another way. In the afternoon, at 15:35 and 15:37, I filmed from the scooter, and the videos show fields of ripe golden rice, some already cut, with jagged limestone ridges behind them and power lines along the road. I do not show the videos. I show a few photos.

I think I stayed in Cao Bằng at Cao Bang Eco Homestay. I am almost sure, but I did not check, and Google Photos' pins, which are estimates, put the evening in Cao Bằng city, near the river. I remember it was nice there. The best part was the restaurant concept: dinner was a barbecue, with a small grill on the table. I do not remember the restaurant's name, so I call it a grill restaurant in the city, and I recommend it. At 21:30 I had a fresh coconut at a table, and Google Photos puts that photo in Cao Bằng city too, as an estimate. I do not remember what the night, the dinner or the coconut cost.

October 15: Bản Giốc and Ngườm Ngao

On October 15 I went to the Bản Giốc waterfall, on the border with China, and to the Ngườm Ngao cave. At the waterfall I did not take the boat. I do not remember what I paid at the gate. I also stopped at a temple on a brick terrace with a view of the valley. I saw no sign there saying that photos or flash are forbidden. On the way I passed a karaoke place and did not go in. That night I think I slept in the same place as the night before, in Cao Bằng, and I remember it was a long ride back from the waterfall. I have not confirmed it.

The cave has a yellow lighting that lights the walls inside. The sign at the entrance is in Vietnamese. Before you go, check the route and the price in the box above, and the closing time, because a source lists it at 17:00.

I saved two screenshots of the route on Google Maps this day. I do not show them, and I do not remember the distance or time.

October 16: Ba Bể lake

On October 16 I reached Ba Bể lake, with wooden boats moored along the shore. I did not take a boat. I took a selfie on the shore, which I do not show. I do not remember where I ate, what I paid to enter, or how long I stayed.

That night I slept in a hotel in Chợ Đồn, by the pin on the next morning's photos. I am almost sure it was Khách Sạn Đức Mạnh, but I did not check it, and I did not find a current listing for it.

October 17: back to Hà Giang

At 08:38 I filmed a shoe-polishing machine in the lobby of that hotel. It worked, and it was funny. From there I rode back to Hà Giang. The ride took the whole day, and I recommend the whole day. By the pins, I passed Chiêm Hóa, in Tuyên Quang province, on the way.

At 10:26 to 10:36 I stopped at a reservoir with a dam. At 12:09 to 12:11 I filmed a river, a bridge and a brick-making site with smoke, which I describe only in words. At 13:50 to 13:59 I stopped at a viewpoint by the 'Welcome to Ha Giang' gate, on road QL2, on the Tuyên Quang side. At 14:01 I photographed a white Catholic church by the river, and at 15:16 a yellow sculpture in a plaza in Vị Xuyên. At 15:35 I filmed a river and a floating restaurant, which I do not name.

I returned the scooter the same day I came back, after 4 to 5 days of rental. At 18:42 I was at a hostel in Hà Giang city, which I do not name. At 19:54 I was on a sleeper bus to Tam Cốc, with pink lights along the ceiling, and that is where the Ninh Bình page begins. I do not remember the price or the arrival time. Today Baolau lists this night bus at 400,000 to 550,000 dong, leaving at 19:30 and arriving at 04:30. I do not remember what the scooter, the petrol, the rooms and the meals cost on these eight days.

What it would cost today

I do not know what the eight days cost me, so I do not give a total. If you want a rough figure, here is my own arithmetic from today's listed prices, not from my trip: a scooter at 7 to 12 euros a day for 4 to 5 days is about 28 to 60 euros. A dorm bed in Cao Bằng averages about 4 to 5 dollars a night, and a homestay about 18. The entrances in the box above come to 25,000 dong for the flag tower, 45,000 for the waterfall and about 45,000 for the cave, so about 115,000 dong, plus a raft if you want one. Petrol and food are on top, and I have no figure for them.

What I recommend, and what I would skip

My view, from these eight days in 2019
  • October 10, Hà Giang: be spontaneous. I walked into a ceremony and a festival by chance, and it was nice.
  • October 12 and 13: I recommend riding alone and meeting good people on the way. That is what happened to me. I met people through a Facebook post, we split, and I got more out of the people who joined by chance. I would skip trying to organize a group through Facebook. Meet people by chance on the road and join them. Check your petrol before a long empty stretch, because we met children whose scooter had run dry.
  • October 14: just flow, and let spirits lead you. I would skip nothing on this day. Have dinner at the grill restaurant if you can find it, because I loved the concept.
  • October 15: come to the waterfalls, and the view in the morning is worth it too. If you are not in a hurry, do not skip anything. I did not take the boat, so I cannot say if it is worth it.
  • October 16: Ba Bể is worth it for the view and the quiet. I did not take a boat.
  • October 17: I recommend everything on this day, the whole ride back. It is long, and it is worth it. The shoe-polishing machine in the lobby really worked.

That is my own view, from 2019.

What I would do differently

I would write down the name of every place I slept, and the price of the scooter, the petrol and the meals. I would also check today whether I need a border permit, and I would ask the hostel before renting. And I would plan less, because the best parts were the unplanned ones.
