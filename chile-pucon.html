South America · Chile · Pucón

Pucón, November 2017

Around November 5 to 7, 2017Guy Haik

Pucón is a lake town in the Araucanía region of southern Chile, at the foot of Villarrica Volcano, and a base for adventure tours. I came from Argentina, after Lago Puelo, and stayed around three days in early November 2017. I rented a quad, saw the waterfalls of Ojos del Caburgua, and climbed to the crater of the volcano.

My conclusion up front: the crater and the lava were impressive, but the tour was too expensive for me, and the quad broke down on the road. Details below.

If you plan to go now: in September 2026 the volcano went to a yellow alert, and at least one local source says summit ascents are not being run. Read the box on the volcano before you book.

The times are my phone's clock, and Google Photos stores them in Chilean time (GMT-3). The boxes hold research I did in November 2026, each with a source and a date, so you can tell it apart from what I remember. Ojos del Caburgua (checked November 2026)

Wikipedia describes the Ojos del Caburgua as a group of waterfalls about 15 km east of Pucón and about 4 km south of Lago Caburgua, near Caburgua, at 110 m above sea level. The page gives no date, and I do not remember if or how much I paid in 2017 (Wikipedia, undated). Check the fee and hours locally.

A travel guide updated on May 15, 2026, written from a 2014 trip, says entry costs 2,000 pesos per person, that you walk on wooden walkways with stairs, and that a bus leaves Pucón's terminal on Uruguay Street at 7:45, 9:00, 10:00, 10:30 and 11:00 for 1,500 pesos one way, with returns from Playa Blanca at 6:30 and 7:20 in the evening (Novo Monde). That is one blog's figures from an older trip, and I did not find an official source. Check them before you go.

Is the crater open now? (checked November 2026)

What I found: on September 4, 2026 Sernageomin raised the technical alert for Villarrica from green to yellow, citing more seismic activity and signs that the lava lake was rising, and Senapred issued a preventive early alert for Pucón, Villarrica, Curarrehue and Panguipulli (Emol, September 5, 2026). A regional news site reported on October 20, 2026 that the yellow alert was still in force (Diario El Ranco). A local guide site, published on October 6 and updated on October 13, 2026, says summit ascents are not currently being run while the rest of Pucón operates normally (El Camino Pucón). Another article, from September 15, 2026, is unclear on this point: it lists a crater trail, but it also labels the summit as restricted or without a trail (Más que Cultura).

What happened last time: in March 2024, with the yellow alert that had been in force since November 2022, a municipal tourism official said the summit was completely closed to everyone (Ladevi, March 4, 2024). The route was reopened on November 22, 2024, after about two years of closure (Cooperativa, November 18, 2024), and on September 1, 2025 the municipality said access up to the summit had no restrictions under a green alert (Municipalidad de Pucón, September 1, 2025).

What I did not find: an official statement about the summit for November 2026. Check CONAF, Sernageomin and Senapred before you book or pay.

Rules, gear and cost (checked November 2026)

Guide: the national tourism site chile.travel, on a page updated on January 7, 2026, says a certified guide is mandatory and that access is often restricted because of volcanic activity or bad weather. It gives the summit height as 2,847 metres and says the drive from Pucón to the access point takes about 30 minutes (chile.travel). A Chilean tourism site, published on May 3, 2026, says the climb must go through agencies registered with Sernatur and certified guides, and that the route takes 4 to 6 hours through snow and volcanic ash (Calendario Chile, May 3, 2026). The local guide site above says CONAF does not allow independent ascents: you need an authorized guide, or you have to show mountain experience. It also says park entry costs CLP 11,000 for a foreign adult, must be bought in advance on pasesparques.cl and is not sold at the gate, and that you register at the CONAF booth in the Rucapillán sector, where the last entry is at 16:00.

The municipal rules: Pucón's adventure-tourism ordinance, approved by Decreto Exento 24 of January 7, 2025, sets a maximum of 12 people per operator on each ascent and one guide for every 1 to 3 passengers, up to three guides and an assistant for 10 to 12. It requires helmets, crampons and ice axes, and a gas filter mask for Villarrica ascents, which matches the mask in my photos, although I do not know what was required in 2017. It also bans night ascents, requires registration at the CONAF booth, and sets a minimum age of 12 with a parent's authorization (Municipalidad de Pucón, ordinance 2025).

Cost: a booking listing with a November 2026 calendar shows the guided ascent at CLP 149,000 per person, 11 hours from 06:30 to 17:30, including pick-up, shared transport, a bilingual guide, park entry, mountain gear, clothing and boots. Food is not included, and the optional chairlift costs CLP 20,000 in cash. The listing says full refund if the tour is cancelled for weather and gives 18 as the minimum age, which differs from the ordinance (Nomades). A travel guide updated on May 15, 2026 says at least 100,000 pesos, a chairlift at 10,000 pesos, and a season from September to June, because the trail is often closed from June to September (Novo Monde). I do not remember what I paid in 2017, so I cannot compare.

What this means for my claim: in my opinion, someone with the right equipment and experience could do it without a tour. The official rule I could not find; the sources above only say that you need a guide or proven mountain experience. Ask CONAF before you plan to go alone.

Leaving Pucón

I left Pucón on a night bus to Santiago, the capital of Chile, and from there I flew to Rosario, in Argentina. I do not remember the date, the bus company, the flight, or what any of it cost.

Night bus to Santiago and flight to Rosario (checked November 2026)

The bus: a bus comparison site, with data found on November 14, 2026, shows about 24 buses a day from Pucón to Santiago, taking roughly 9 to 12 hours. Many leave between about 19:00 and 22:00 and arrive between about 6:00 and 8:00 the next morning, so the night bus is a common way to do it. The companies include Turbus, Pullman Bus, Buses JAC, Buses Jet Sur, Buses Transantin and FlixBus, and the prices run from about 13 to 64 US dollars (CheckMyBus). I do not know which company I took or what I paid.

The flight: a flight comparison page, for November 17, 2026, lists a direct Aerolíneas Argentinas flight from Rosario to Santiago, with tickets from about 199 (the page does not show the currency) (Omio). That is the opposite direction from mine, and I did not check my route, so look up Santiago to Rosario separately.

What I think now

Pucón is easy to like: a lake, a volcano, forests and waterfalls close to the town. The crater and the lava are worth seeing, but the price was too high for me, so if you go, compare prices first. I would not rent a quad from an agency I had not checked, and if I did I would test it before riding far from town. The Ojos del Caburgua are worth the ride.
