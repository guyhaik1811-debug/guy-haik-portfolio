Puerto Princesa and Sabang, September 2019

From September 21, 2019Guy Haik

I landed in Puerto Princesa, on the island of Palawan, on the night of September 20, 2019, on the flight from Manila that the previous page ends with. This page covers the next two days, September 21 and 22: a day on a rented scooter around the city, a visit to the crocodile center and a ride north to the village of Sabang. It is built from my photos, my phone's clock and what I remember, and I say plainly where I do not remember.

In short: two days on a scooter, one visit I would not repeat, and a long ride at the end of the second day. The times are my phone's clock, shown by Google Photos in GMT+08:00. Google Photos pins the places, and I say where a pin is only a rough area. The boxes hold what I looked up in November 2026, each marked as current data and not as a 2019 price.

What to know today, in short (checked November 2026)
  • Scooter rental: sources disagree widely. Older guides mention about 450 to 700 pesos a day. One rental company lists 1,400 to 1,800 pesos a day for its motorbikes. Expect a cash deposit of up to 5,000 pesos.
  • Licence: current rental sites ask for a motorcycle licence, or an international driving permit with it. I was not asked for one in 2019, as far as I remember.
  • Crocodile center: the sources I found disagree about the fee. One lists 40 pesos for an adult, another says entry is free. Check before you go.
  • Puerto Princesa to Sabang: about 70 km. Google Maps shows about 1 hour 30 minutes by car, and the city's own park site says about an hour and a half by public transport.
  • My own 2019 prices: I do not remember any, so I give none.

The sources are in the box at the end. These things change, so check before you go.

September 21: a scooter and the city

I slept in a hostel very close to the airport, and I walked to it from the airport. I do not remember its name, so I do not give one. I rented a scooter at the hostel. I do not remember the price or how long I rented it for. I was not asked for a licence, as far as I remember. I have a motorcycle licence and an international driving permit, so it did not matter for me, but see the box at the end for what rental sites ask for today.

At 14:58 I stopped and took a photo of myself in a red helmet next to the scooter's mirror, in front of a bamboo fence.

At 15:07 and 15:08 I took two photos of a mangrove shore at low tide, with small fishing boats in the distance. Google Photos' pin is near Canigaran Beach, as an estimate, and I do not remember exactly where I stopped. The second photo shows rows of low concrete steps on the mud. I do not know what they are, so I do not guess.

At 17:50 I was on the Baywalk, the promenade along the harbor in the city. Google Photos names the place "Puerto Princesa City Baywalk Park." I took a selfie on the sea wall, with the fishing boats in the water behind me.

At 19:14 I photographed the lit sign that reads "I ♥ PUERTO PRINCESA" on the promenade, with palm trees and street lamps behind it and an empty road in front. I have one more photo of myself with my helmet in a fast-food place, which I do not show, and I do not remember what I ate.

After midnight, between 00:48 and 00:54, I filmed three short videos in the street: bright lights at the end of a wide road, and later fireworks behind trees. I do not know what the event was, and I do not know the exact place. I do not show the videos, because they are dark and tell you nothing.

September 22: the crocodile center

On the afternoon of September 22 I rode the scooter to the Palawan Wildlife Rescue and Conservation Center, which most people call the crocodile farm. Google Photos names it "Palawan Wildlife Rescue and Conservation Center (Crocodile Farm)," and my photos from it run from 14:33 to 15:41. There was no guide, and I walked around by myself. I do not remember what I paid at the entrance, or whether I paid.

My view, plainly: I would not do it again. I am against keeping animals in cages so that people can watch them, and this place is built around that. The photos below are what I saw. I do not show other visitors or staff.

I photographed a sign that says "Do not buy any pet species caught from the wild." It cites the Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act, Republic Act 9147, and shows photos of animals, among them crocodiles and birds. Inside I also photographed the plan of the site, which lists the animals as leopard cat, bearcat group, hornbill, parrot, mynah, serpent eagle and sea eagle. A small sign next to it asks visitors not to feed the animals.

Most of what I photographed were birds in big mesh cages: a pair of hornbills, and a white-bellied sea eagle with its information sign. I do not know the species of the hornbills. I have no photo of a sign for them, and the site plan names them only "hornbill."

At 15:26 I photographed a bearcat (also called a binturong) lying on a wooden platform. That is the name the center's own plan uses.

At 15:34 I photographed a roadside banner for "Crocodile Sisig," a dish of chopped crocodile meat, with the words "Available here." The banner says it is a product of a farm that raises crocodiles, and lists "health benefits" such as "good for asthma," "good for heart" and "aphrodisiac." Those are the banner's claims, and I did not check them.

There is a covered area with a crocodile skin on the wall and a skeleton in a glass case, and a table of skulls of different sizes under a yellow "Conservation Center" sign, with two huge bones forming an arch over it. Next to a dish of eggs was a printed card about sperm whales, so I think the big bones come from a whale, but I did not read the other cards and I do not know where they came from.

The young crocodiles are kept in shallow concrete tanks under a wire grid. I took several photos of them. In one tank they lie in a pile and in another one swims alone. In a darker tank a large crocodile lies in the corner of the water.

September 22: the ride to Sabang

After the center I rode north. Google Maps puts the road from the center to Sabang Beach at about 70 km, and about 1 hour 30 minutes by car (checked in November 2026, so it is today's traffic). I do not know how long it took me on a scooter, and I do not remember where I stopped on the way or whether the scooter was allowed on that road.

At 18:02 I photographed the road from the seat. A sign at the road says "STOP for inspection" and another says "SLOW DOWN." It carries the letters PAMB and PPSRNP, which stand for the Protected Area Management Board and the Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park, the protected area that includes the underground river (Puerto Princesa Underground River). Google Photos' pin is on this road near a point it calls "Cockatoo Lookout." I did not write down the name of the place.

I think I spent the evening in Sabang, the small village by the beach next to the park. At 20:57 I photographed a street food sign for a shawarma at 80 pesos, a 2019 price. I do not know where the stall was, and Google Photos pins it only as Puerto Princesa City. At 23:44 I photographed a chocolate shake at a bar, and Google Photos' pin for that one is at Sabang Beach. I also photographed a drink served in a whole green coconut with a paper umbrella and a cherry.

I think I slept near Sabang that night. I do not have a photo of the hostel, and I do not remember its name. I also have no photo of the underground river, so I do not say whether I visited it.

What I looked up for this stretch (checked November 2026)
  • Scooter rental prices: a Philippine rental company lists 1,400 to 1,800 pesos a day for its motorbikes (Philippine Travel Guides). An older guide says most agencies asked 450 to 500 pesos a day for a scooter in 2017, and that one charged 649 (Enidhi). A guide of unknown date says about 700 pesos a day (Hoteles.com).
  • Licence and deposit: one rental guide lists a passport, a valid motorcycle licence with an English translation or an international driving permit, and a cash deposit of 5,000 pesos that is refunded (Philippine Travel Guides). A review reply on a ticket site describes another operator's contract with the same 5,000 pesos deposit, a limit of 100 km a day and use only inside the city proper (Klook). I did not check what my own rental had.
  • Crocodile center: one guide lists 8:00 to 4:45 and 40 pesos for an adult, 30 for a senior and 20 for a child (Philippine Beaches). Another site lists the same place as free, open 8:00 to 5:00 (Forever Vacation). They do not agree, so check on the day. Wikipedia says it opened in 1987 and is in Irawan (Wikipedia).
  • City to Sabang: the city's park site says the park is reached by a 76 km concrete road, served by jeepneys and air-conditioned vans, and that the trip takes about an hour and a half (Puerto Princesa Underground River). The same site says everyone needs an entry permit from the booking office before entering the park. I did not look up the permit prices.
  • What I did not find: any 2019 price for my scooter or my hostel, because I do not remember them.

These are prices and notes as listed today, not what I paid.
