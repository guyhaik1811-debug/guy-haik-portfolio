Tabatinga to Manaus by slow boat in 2026: what to check before you go, and what the trip was like

November 2026Guy Haik

I took the slow boat from Tabatinga, on the Brazilian side of the border with Colombia, to Manaus in December 2017. I left on December 22 and arrived on the night of December 25. That was nine years ago, so this is partly what I remember and photographed, and partly what I checked again in November 2026. The checked facts are marked with a source and a date. I did not find an official page or timetable for the boats, so every figure below comes from travel blogs and guides, and I say which ones disagree.

The trip

I flew into Leticia, in Colombia, stayed one night and walked about half an hour to Tabatinga, a Brazilian town that runs straight into Leticia. There they pay in reais and speak Portuguese. At a stall I asked on the spur of the moment when there was a boat to Manaus. The answer was: "Today. Buy a hammock and come."

I stopped a man on a motorbike, and he took me to a place that sold hammocks. I bought the cheapest one, and I do not recommend that. I was also asked to go to the police station in Tabatinga to get my passport stamped, and I went there on the same motorbike. I do not remember what the ticket, the hammock or the ride cost.

I slept three nights in a hammock on an open deck under a ceiling covered in orange life jackets. Food was included in the ticket. There was a small canteen on the top deck where you could buy things like peanuts. There was a small shower stall and a toilet that sends everything straight into the river. I think the shower water came from a tank, and I do not remember whether men and women were separated. It was clean enough, and "enough" is relative.

The river is wide and brown, and the banks are a low green line most of the time. The sky changed all day, from heavy clouds in the afternoon to the sun low over the water in the evening.

We reached Manaus in the night between December 25 and 26. People from the boat helped me find a taxi at that hour, and I went straight to a hostel and to sleep. My advice is to use a ride app instead.

What to check before you go (November 2026)

  • The fare. A guide published on April 30, 2026 and last modified on September 27, 2026 says the slow boat from Tabatinga to Manaus cost 290 reais in hammock class in mid-April 2026, and the fast boat 1,010 reais (nomadicbackpacker.com). A guide published in September 2022 gave 240 reais for the hammock, 1,200 for a private air-conditioned cabin and 910 for the fast boat (Por um Recomeço, September 6, 2022). I found no cabin price for 2026.
  • How long it takes. The 2026 guide says 78 hours for the author, about four days and three nights. The 2022 guide says three to four days, and another guide published in May 2022 and modified in May 2024 says about 72 hours (earthmaho.com). The fast boat is about 30 hours. Mine was about three days.
  • When it leaves. The 2026 guide says slow boats do not run every day and that delays and cancellations happen. The 2022 guide gives departures from Tabatinga on Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday at 12:00, and says to be at the port by 9:00 Manaus time. The 2026 guide says the fast boat leaves daily at 10:00. My boat left in the late afternoon: it was still at the pier at 15:31 and my video of the river as we left is from 17:46. Treat all of these as approximate and confirm them at the port.
  • Where to buy. At the river terminal in Tabatinga, which is not open around the clock (nomadicbackpacker.com, 2026). The 2022 guide says to buy at the official ticket office and not from agents who approach you on board, who charged more. A 2026 estimate page for boats at Manaus also says to use official ticket booths, to carry some cash because card and Pix payments may fail with weak signal, and that a port fee may be charged separately at the turnstile (veredictomanauara.com.br, no date, titled 2026). That page is about the Manaus end.
  • The hammock. The boat does not provide one. The 2026 guide says 40 reais for the cheapest, with 10 more for rope, and 40 to 50 in its FAQ, and says to hang it away from the bathrooms and the television. The May 2022 guide says about 10 US dollars with two ropes. The Manaus page says to bring a sturdy rope, a light sheet and a jacket, since the wind mid-river is cold at night. I bought the cheapest one and do not recommend it.
  • Meals and water. The 2026 guide says three meals a day were included (breakfast about 6:30, lunch at 11:00, dinner at 17:00), that you bring your own plate, cup and cutlery, that filtered drinking water was free, and that alcohol was not allowed. The May 2022 guide found the food tasty but repetitive.
  • Showers and toilets. The 2026 guide says the bathrooms were separate for men and women and that the showers used semi-filtered river water. The May 2022 guide says the bathrooms were cleaned often, that the toilet water comes from the river and looks brownish, and that the men's area was much worse. Accounts differ, so I would not count on any one description.
  • Security and valuables. The 2022 guide says to keep money and valuables in a bag you carry, to lock your luggage near the hammock, and to watch your bags at every port stop. The May 2022 guide reported no problems but says to keep the daypack in the hammock at night and to take turns watching the bags. It also says outlets for charging are few and shared.
  • The border stamps. The 2026 guide says you get your exit stamp at the immigration office at Leticia airport, since Leticia has no immigration office in town, and then your entry stamp at the Polícia Federal in Tabatinga within 24 hours. The 2022 guide also mentions an online form for Colombia, and describes a police check, a wristband and a sniffer-dog search at the Tabatinga port before boarding. I was asked to go to the police station in Tabatinga to get my passport stamped, and I do not remember an exit stamp in Leticia. Leticia, Tabatinga and the Peruvian town of Santa Rosa have no hard border, so you can walk between them (nomadicbackpacker.com, 2026). Tabatinga is one hour ahead of Leticia (2022 guide). My own phone's clock stayed on Colombian time, an hour behind the local time that Google Photos shows for the same photos.
  • Arrival. The 2026 author docked in Manaus at about 19:00 and walked to a hotel. My last photo of the boat at the dock in Manaus is from about 01:36. The arrival hour varies, so plan a way to your bed that does not depend on finding a taxi at night. Uber says its app works around the clock in Manaus (uber.com, 2026 copyright, no page date), but I found no fare from the port.

What I could not check

I found no official timetable or price list from the boat companies or the port, nothing at all on M. Fernandes in 2026, and no 2026 price for a cabin. The figures above come from a few independent bloggers across 2022 to 2026, and prices have moved between them. I also did not check the visa rules for your passport, or any travel advisory for the tri-border area of Colombia, Brazil and Peru. Check these with the port, your embassy and your government's travel advice before you go.

What I would tell a friend

Three things from my own trip. Do not buy the cheapest hammock. Food is included, so you do not need to plan meals. And in Manaus, use a ride app instead of looking for a taxi at night.
