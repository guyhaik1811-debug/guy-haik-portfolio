Nacpan and El Nido, September 24 to 27, 2019

September 24 to 27, 2019Guy Haik

From September 24 to 27, 2019 I stayed on the northwest coast of Palawan. I took a bus from Port Barton to Nacpan Beach, slept in a hostel there, rode a scooter to the town of El Nido, and on September 26 I swam, for a few hours, to an island beach, and paid for a boat back. On September 27 I left by bus, and I then flew to Manila and on to Siargao. I would not swim it again.

In short: three days, one long swim, and a lot of beach. The swim is the story. I took no photos while I was in the water, so I say what I know and what I do not. The times are my phone's clock, shown by Google Photos in GMT+08:00. The places are Google Photos' pins, and I say where a pin is an estimate. I leave out the hostel, the restaurant, the faces of other people and every video with people.

What to know today, in short (checked November 2026)
  • Port Barton to Nacpan: Google Maps shows 185 km and about 3 hours 47 minutes by car. Route sites list a van at roughly 3.5 to 4 hours and about 710 pesos, and a bus at about 4.5 hours and about 410 pesos, but they disagree and one lists up to 5,500 pesos, which looks like a private car. Treat these as a range and ask locally.
  • Nacpan to El Nido town: about 17 km and 45 minutes by road, according to one hostel's guide. A scooter costs about 400 to 600 pesos a day plus fuel, and a shared van about 150 to 200 pesos one way, as one site lists them. A tricycle from town to Nacpan is listed at 500 pesos for one person on a hostel page.
  • Nacpan to Puerto Princesa: a van takes about 5 to 6 hours, with about 11 departures a day between 5:00 and 17:30 on one booking site, from about 12 US dollars. The 2019 board in the photo below shows four van times a day.
  • Seven Commando Beach: it is on the main island, at the edge of El Nido's bay, and the straight-line distance from El Nido Beach is about 750 meters. You cannot walk there because of the cliffs. Most people arrive on an island-hopping tour, usually Tour A, or by kayak.
  • El Nido to Puerto Princesa: Google Maps shows 271 km and about 4 hours 55 minutes by car, via the Puerto Princesa North Road. Google Maps lists public-transport departures from the El Nido bus stop at 5:00, 7:00, 7:30, 9:00 and 10:00, among others. A booking site lists the first van at 4:00 and the last at 20:30, travel time of 5 to 6 hours, and fares of about 600 pesos (regular bus) to 900 pesos (elite bus) for a bus or van. Vans only go to the town or the airport, and the airport bus terminal can be far from the airport itself.
  • Flying on: Priceline lists direct flights from Puerto Princesa to Manila at about 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes. Expedia lists an average of about 2 hours 5 minutes from Manila to Siargao. I give no fares, because they vary a lot.
  • What it costs to go to Seven Commando today: kayaks are about 300 to 400 pesos an hour at El Nido Beach. A tour operator lists an eco-tourism fee of 200 pesos a person, plus 200 for each of the lagoons, on top of the tour. I did not find a price for a boat to this beach alone, and I do not remember what I paid in 2019, so I give none.

The sources are in the box at the end. These prices change, so check before you go.

September 24: the bus to Nacpan

I went from Port Barton to Nacpan by bus. I do not remember when it left, what it cost or where I got off. I only know the distance and time from Google Maps today, and they are in the box above. I slept in a hostel at Nacpan, and I do not give its name.

In the evening I photographed a sailboat with the name *Perfect Storm* painted on its hull, pulled up on the sand under palm trees. Google Photos' pin for the evening photos is near Nacpan and Twin Beach.

That night I filmed a short video in a restaurant near Nacpan. It was a nice restaurant. I do not give its name and I do not show the video or the diners. Google Photos places the video only as an estimate near Nacpan.

September 25: the scooter, El Nido town and Lio

I do not remember where I slept on the nights of the 24th and the 25th. My last photo of each night is pinned at Nacpan, so it was probably there. I moved around by scooter, between Nacpan, El Nido town and Lio, and the day I remember is a walk around. I have no photos from before 16:09.

At 16:09 I photographed El Nido's bay from above the rooftops. Google Photos pins this to Barangay Buena Suerte, in El Nido town. The bay was full of outrigger boats.

At 16:54 I was at Lio Beach, north of the town. The photos show a long wooden pier, and a large sign that spells LIO in the sand. The pin is near the Lio Beach and the El Nido airport. I also took two selfies there, and I do not show them.

At 23:28 I was back at Nacpan. The pin is on Nacpan Beach, near Twin Beach, and I photographed the night sign that reads I ♥ NACPAN.

September 26: the swim

At 11:56 I photographed a board that listed the vans from Nacpan to Puerto Princesa, which is called PPS on the board. It lists four departure windows a day: 5:00 to 6:00 and 8:00 to 9:00 in the morning, and 2:00 to 3:00 and 5:00 to 6:00 in the afternoon. There was no price on it. I photographed only the board, and the person behind it is not shown.

I then rode the scooter to El Nido town and started from the town's beach. My first photos from there are from 13:51. Google Photos' pin for the afternoon is in El Nido, but I did not check these pins one by one.

Someone offered to take me to an island by boat, for a fee, and told me a James Bond film had been shot on it. I decided to swim instead. It took a few hours, and I took no photos on the way. I was very tired afterwards, and looking back I would not do it again.

I do not know exactly which way I swam. One source puts the straight-line distance from El Nido Beach to this beach at about 750 meters, so I cannot say how far I really swam, and I did not measure it. I do not recommend this swim. If you want to go there, the section above says how people do it today.

My first photos from the island are from 17:19. They show a white beach under a cliff with a hole through the rock. I do not know which film was shot there, and I found no source that names the beach, so I do not name one.

A dog stayed around me on the island. I do not know whether it came with someone or lived there. It spent a long time in the shallow water, and I photographed it several times between 17:27 and 17:36.

From about 17:46 I took photos of a beach with brown sand and dense forest, and of the sunset. At 17:57 I photographed a sign that reads Seven Commando Beach. I think the man who rented out boats there told me that was the island's name. He saw me and told me that nobody had ever done what I did.

The way back, by boat

I paid for a boat back, and I do not remember the price. It took me to the beach I had started from, in El Nido. My photos from the boat are from 17:59 to 18:18. They show the coast with a few thatched houses on the shore, a pink sky and then, at 18:18, the lights of the town at night.

I also have a photo of a menu from an Israeli-style restaurant. I do not know when I took it. It matches the menu of Happiness Beach Bar on Serena Street in El Nido town, according to the sources below, though the menu in my photo has no name on it, so I do not say for certain that I ate there. I do not show it, because its prices are from 2019. I do not know where I slept that night or how I got back to Nacpan, if I did.

September 27: the way back

I left El Nido by bus, heading back toward Puerto Princesa. I do not remember when it left, where I got on or what I paid. Today Google Maps lists departures from 5:00 in the morning, and the booking site gives about 600 to 900 pesos, so my bus was probably one of the morning ones, though I cannot say which. The box above has the details.

My only photo from the day is from 15:00. Google Photos' pin is in Roxas, Palawan, on the road near a high school, so I was on the way south. I took it through a window: a man carrying a net cage with an animal in it. I think it was a pig, and I do not know where it was going. I cropped the photo so that no one's face shows.

I did not sleep in El Nido that night: on September 27 I flew on, from Puerto Princesa to Manila and then to Siargao. I do not remember the flight times, the airlines or the fares, and I do not remember where I spent the night. By 18:43 on September 28 I was in Siargao, which starts the next chapter.

What I would do differently

I would not swim it again. I was very tired afterwards. That is the only thing from these three days that I know I would change.

Sources and what I checked (November 2026)

  • Costs today: The Backpacking Family lists the kayak price per hour. Discover El Nido lists the eco-tourism and lagoon fees. I did not find a current price for a boat to this beach alone.
  • The film: Outlook Traveller says Palawan was featured in films including a James Bond one. I did not find a source that names the beach or the film.
  • The way back: 12Go, El Nido to Puerto Princesa lists the vans and the fare. Priceline lists the Puerto Princesa to Manila flight time, and Expedia the Manila to Siargao flight time. The 271 km and 4 hours 55 minutes are Google Maps, checked in November 2026.

These are prices and notes as listed today, not what I paid.
