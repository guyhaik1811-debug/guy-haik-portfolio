Villarrica Volcano climb from Pucón: is it open, and what to check before you book

November 2026Guy Haik

I climbed to the crater of Villarrica, the volcano above the lake town of Pucón in southern Chile, in early November 2017. That was nine years ago, so this is partly what I remember and partly what I checked again in November 2026. If you plan to go now, start with the box on the alert, because things have changed since September.

The climb

I booked through my hostel, and it cost a lot of money. In my opinion it was not worth that price, and I do not remember the exact figure. I reached the crater rim at the top. I wore a helmet and a gas mask, and from the rim I could see lava glowing at the bottom of the crater. I do not remember the start time, how many hours it took, how big the group was, or what gear came with the tour. The photos from the top did not come from my phone, so I do not show them here.

Is the crater open now? (November 2026)

The alert

What I found: on September 4, 2026 the national geology service, Sernageomin, raised the technical alert for Villarrica from green to yellow, citing more seismic activity and signs that the lava lake was rising, and the emergency agency, Senapred, issued a preventive early alert for Pucón, Villarrica, Curarrehue and Panguipulli (Emol, September 5, 2026). A regional news site reported on October 20, 2026 that the yellow alert was still in force (Diario El Ranco, October 20, 2026). A local guide site, published on October 6 and updated on October 13, 2026, says summit ascents are not currently being run while the rest of Pucón operates normally (El Camino Pucón). Another article, from September 15, 2026, is unclear: it lists a crater trail, but it also labels the summit as restricted or without a trail (Más que Cultura, September 15, 2026).

What happened last time: in March 2024, with the yellow alert that had been in force since November 2022, a municipal tourism official said the summit was completely closed to everyone (Ladevi, March 4, 2024). The route was reopened on November 22, 2024, after about two years (Cooperativa, November 18, 2024), and on September 1, 2025 the municipality said access up to the summit had no restrictions under a green alert (Municipalidad de Pucón, September 1, 2025).

What I did not find: an official statement about the summit for November 2026, and anything newer than October 20. Sernageomin's own site would not open for me. Check CONAF, Sernageomin and Senapred, or ask the agency in writing, before you book or pay.

Rules, gear and cost

  • A guide. The national tourism site says a certified guide is mandatory and that access is often restricted because of volcanic activity or bad weather. It gives the summit height as 2,847 metres and the drive from Pucón to the access point as about 30 minutes (chile.travel). A Chilean tourism site says the climb must go through agencies registered with Sernatur and certified guides, and takes 4 to 6 hours through snow and volcanic ash (Calendario Chile, May 3, 2026). The local guide site says CONAF does not allow independent ascents: you need an authorized guide, or you have to show mountain experience.
  • Park entry. The same local guide says entry costs CLP 11,000 for a foreign adult, must be bought in advance on pasesparques.cl and is not sold at the gate. You register at the CONAF booth in the Rucapillán sector, and the last entry is at 16:00.
  • Municipal rules. Pucón's adventure-tourism ordinance, approved by Decreto Exento 24 of January 7, 2025, sets a maximum of 12 people per operator on each ascent and one guide for every 1 to 3 passengers. It requires helmets, crampons, ice axes and a gas filter mask for Villarrica, and bans night ascents (Municipalidad de Pucón, ordinance 2025). I do not know what was required in 2017.
  • Cost. A booking listing with a November 2026 calendar shows the guided ascent at CLP 149,000 per person, 11 hours from 06:30 to 17:30, including pick-up, shared transport, a bilingual guide, park entry, mountain gear and boots. Food is not included, and the optional chairlift costs CLP 20,000 in cash. It offers a full refund if the tour is cancelled for weather (Nomades). A travel guide updated on May 15, 2026 says at least 100,000 pesos, a chairlift at 10,000 pesos, and a season from September to June (Novo Monde). I do not remember what I paid, so I cannot compare.

Can you do it alone?

In my opinion, someone with the right equipment and experience could climb without a tour. I could not find an official rule that says so, and the sources above only say that you need a guide or proven mountain experience. Ask CONAF before you plan to go alone.

Is it worth it?

The crater and the lava were impressive, but the tour was too expensive for me, so compare prices before you pay. And since the alert is yellow right now, the first question is whether the summit is being climbed at all. If it is closed, the town still has lakes, forests and waterfalls close by.
