Machu Picchu in 2026: tickets, prices and walking up from Aguas Calientes, and what to check before you go

November 2026Guy Haik

I went to Machu Picchu on October 6, 2017. That was nine years ago, so this is partly what I remember and photographed, and partly what I checked again in November 2026. One warning first: I could not confirm one number, the foreigner ticket price, because my sources disagree. I say so below.

The day

I left Aguas Calientes very early and walked up to the ruins instead of taking the bus. It was about a thousand steps, probably more, and it was very hard. I did not count them and I did not write down the time I left. I was among the ruins by about 06:07. The site stayed in cloud for the next two hours, the paths were almost empty, and a llama stood on one of the walls above the path.

I only saw the postcard view for the first time at 08:08, and then the grass terraces turned bright green. After that I climbed one of the two mountains that stand beside the site, between about 08:35 and 10:40. I no longer remember which one, and I entered the site without a guide.

By noon the main viewpoint was packed with visitors. The quietest hours I saw were between 06:00 and 08:00.

What to check before you go (November 2026)

  • Until the end of 2026 the sanctuary fee is waived. On November 14, 2026 the park agency, SERNANP, set the entry fee to the Historic Sanctuary of Machu Picchu at zero soles through December 31, 2026 (it was 11 soles for foreign adults). It does not waive the citadel ticket, which you buy from the Ministry of Culture, and it does not cover those who enter through the Inca Trail (Infobae, November 15, 2026). The extra 11-soles fee had been announced earlier for May 2026 (Infomercado, February 5, 2026).
  • The ticket price for foreigners. The same November 15 article puts the citadel ticket for the regular routes (circuits 1, 2 and 3) at 152 soles for an adult. An Infobae article from November 18, 2025 lists US$60 for a foreign adult in 2026, which does not match, so confirm the price on tuboleto.cultura.pe before you plan. The official 2026 table lists a "general rate" of 64 soles for most routes and 112 soles for the mountain routes, but it does not say who pays it, and the November 15 article gives 112 soles as a rate for Peruvians, so I would not read those as the foreign prices (Infobae, November 1, 2025). I did not find a foreigner price for the mountains.
  • The daily limit. 5,600 visitors from June 19 to November 2, 2026 and 4,500 on the other dates. Tickets are sold on the official site, tuboleto.cultura.pe (Andina, November 3, 2025). They are personal, and the name must match your document. You can also buy in person in Machu Picchu Pueblo from 15:00 to 22:00, but those tickets are only valid the next day (Infobae, November 18, 2025).
  • Entry windows and a guide. Each ticket has an exact entry hour and does not allow entry at another time, and you stay about 4 to 6 hours, depending on the route. Two tour-operator pages say a guide is not mandatory for the citadel circuits, though one recommends it (How To Peru, January 22, 2026; TR Experience Peru, April 21, 2026). They disagree on the maximum stay, so read what is printed on your ticket.
  • Walking up from Aguas Calientes. A guide to the footpath says the walk up takes about an hour and a half, a little less coming down, on a stone stepped path. It rates it moderate, says you do not need a ticket for the path itself, and warns not to walk after dark, around 6 pm. In the wet season, November to April, it is slippery (The Only Peru Guide, page modified September 25, 2026). I found it much harder than moderate, and I started very early.
  • The mountains. A trekking agency's guide to Huayna Picchu says it allows 350 people a day, in two groups of 175, and takes about three hours for the round trip (Salkantay Trekking, updated May 4, 2026). I do not know which mountain I climbed, so this is only research.

What I could not check

I could not confirm the foreigner ticket price, so check tuboleto.cultura.pe. I also did not find a foreigner price for the mountains, the exact time I left Aguas Calientes, or what I paid for the day in 2017.

Is it worth it?

Yes, but go early. Even at dawn the view only opened at 08:08, and by noon the viewpoint was crowded. I would walk up again if I were fit, and I would take the bus if I were not, because the climb is hard. Start early and take it slowly, and check the ticket price in advance.
