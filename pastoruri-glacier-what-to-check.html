Pastoruri Glacier near Huaraz: what is left to see, and what to check before you go

November 2026Guy Haik

I visited the Pastoruri glacier on Saturday, September 23, 2017, on a full-day trip from Huaraz. That was nine years ago and the glacier has kept shrinking, so this is partly what I saw and partly what I checked again in November 2026.

The day

I went with an agency, which is the normal way. Huaraz is full of them. The road climbs through high, open country, with a few stops on the way: a local woman with her alpacas, a small pool in the high wetland. From the end of the road, a paved path leads towards the glacier. At the end there is a lake below the glacier front, with blocks of ice in the water. Nothing about the day was hard for me.

Then and now

I saw the glacier in 2017, and it has been shrinking for decades. According to Peru's national water authority (ANA), as reported by Actualidad Ambiental in October 2024, Pastoruri lost about 60% of its ice surface between 1995 and 2016, and its front retreated about 23 metres a year between 1980 and 2019 (Actualidad Ambiental, October 30, 2024). Expect less ice than in my photos.

What to check before you go (November 2026)

  • The price. A group trip from Huaraz is listed at about $25 per person, with pick-up around 8:30 (Huillca Expedition). Another guide suggests 25 to 30 soles for a last-minute booking, starting at 9:00 and returning around 18:00 (Laid Back Trip). The two do not agree, so ask each agency what is included.
  • What is not usually included. The park ticket (30 soles for one day for foreigners, per Trámites Perú, updated July 29, 2026), lunch (optional, about 30 soles) and horse hire for part of the path.
  • The walk and the altitude. About 40 minutes from the end of the road to the glacier. Sources give different altitudes, from about 4,800 m at the car park to 5,000 to 5,250 m at the glacier, so plan for thin air either way. One guide suggests doing the glacier near the end of your stay, after a few days of acclimatisation.
  • Doing it alone. One guide says there is currently no public transport to the glacier area, so the options are a tour or a private taxi. I could not find a taxi price.
  • Access. In April 2024 local authorities asked agencies to suspend trips to this sector while a damaged bridge was repaired (El Tiempo, April 12, 2024). Later reports say it reopened: more than 5,000 people visited over the Fiestas Patrias weekend in 2025 (Andina, August 8, 2025). Still, ask your agency or hostel whether the route is open on your dates.

One thing I would not do

I do not recommend hiring a horse for part of the path. I am against using animals so that tourists do not have to walk, and I would rather you walk at your own pace.

Is it worth it?

I have not been back, so I cannot tell you what it looks like now. If seeing a glacier is the reason to go, know that there is less ice every year. If you are already in Huaraz with a free day and you are comfortable at altitude, it is a simple trip, and it was for me.
