Is Laguna Parón open? What I found about access, checked November 2026

November 2026Guy Haik

I visited Laguna Parón on Monday, September 25, 2017, on a day trip from Huaraz with an agency. Back then it was an ordinary tour. Since then the situation has changed, and that is the more useful thing to tell you, so most of this article is what I found, with dates and sources, in November 2026. I have not been back, and I could not verify anything on site.

The short answer

As of the latest reports I found, tourist visits to the Parón ravine have been restricted since 2023, and the restriction appears to still be in place. Please do not plan a trip from this page. Check the current status with the Huascarán National Park authority first.

What happened, by date

  • July 2023. The park authority (SERNANP) and the glacier institute INAIGEM announced a closure, after INAIGEM rated the danger around the lake as "very high" from an avalanche or falling rock that could cause an overflow (Andina, July 21, 2023).
  • June 2024. The consumer authority Indecopi fined two Huaraz agencies for offering Parón visits despite the warning (Andina, June 14, 2024).
  • July 10, 2026. The park chief said entry remains restricted, that people still reach the lake anyway, and that there is no permanent control post there (Ancash Noticias).
  • August 30, 2026. El Comercio reported a level of 4,189.69 m, above the 4,185 m reference maximum, and cracks in the dike according to an unnamed local worker. The same article says the local community president disputes that there is any danger (El Comercio).
  • October 12, 2026. The Environment Minister called on the authorities to prevent the risk of a flood. INAIGEM estimates that more than 22,000 people in Caraz could be affected (Chimbote en Línea). The reports I read do not say whether the visiting rules changed.

If an agency sells you a Parón tour as if nothing has changed, treat that as a warning sign.

What I saw in 2017

I did not keep notes on the times or the route, so I will not invent them. I have photos from about 11:40 to 12:50. The first viewpoint, with the summits partly in cloud and a small boat on the shore. A walk down the valley track. The best views came last, from a pile of granite blocks high above the water. Under a brighter sky, the lake looked far more turquoise than it did an hour earlier.

About the lake

Parón is a glacial lake in Huascarán National Park, about 32 km east of Caraz. It is roughly 3.7 km long and 700 m wide on average, and it sits among peaks that reach about 6,400 m. Sources disagree on the surface altitude: Wikipedia gives 4,155 m today and 4,185 m originally, and travel guides usually say about 4,200 m. Its level is regulated through a tunnel and an underwater gate, and the 2026 reports are about who operates and maintains those works (Wikipedia, Lake Parón).

What I would do

Find out first whether the visit is allowed at all, from the park authority and not from the agency selling it. If it is closed, use the day for something that is open. I visited Pastoruri two days before Parón, and I wrote what to check before you go there in a separate article.
