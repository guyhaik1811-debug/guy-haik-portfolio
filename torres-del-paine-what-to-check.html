Torres del Paine in 2026: tickets, rules and what the trek was really like

November 2026Guy Haik

I spent three days on foot in Torres del Paine National Park, from Wednesday, October 25 to Friday, October 27, 2017, with a shuttle in and a bus back to Puerto Natales. That was nine years ago, so this is partly what I remember and photographed, and partly what I checked again in November 2026.

My conclusion up front: in hindsight I would not do it again. It is mostly for people who love trekking. The views are beautiful, but three days is a lot of walking if you come for the scenery and not for the walking itself, and it rained on two of my three days.

What I did

Day one. I walked from the Paine Grande area to Grey Glacier and back, all in one day, which is long. The first view of Lago Grey, with floating ice at the far end, was the best moment, and at the end of the trail there were blue icebergs in front of the glacier.

I got lost on the way back. I do not remember where on the trail or for how long. I found my way again before dark, and I was lucky. If I could give one piece of advice about this park, it would be: do not walk it alone if you can avoid it. Go with a guide, or at least in a group. The trails can split, the weather changes fast, and nobody may be close enough to hear you.

Days two and three. I slept in a tent the first night and in a bed in a hostel room the second, after the rain. On the second day the path ran along a long turquoise lake under heavy cloud and wind, and on the third I walked out along the same lake, which was as still as glass.

What to check before you go (November 2026)

  • The ticket system. From May 1, 2026, the old fee based on staying fewer or more than three days was replaced by two tickets: a single-day entry, and a multi-day entry for 2 to 10 days (CNN Chile, around April 29, 2026). The change had been planned for January and was postponed so it would not affect the summer season (Meganoticias, April 30, 2026).
  • The price. A guide that says its prices were checked in September 2026 on the official ticketing portal lists US$36.16 for a foreign adult for one day and US$54.13 for 2 to 10 days (Wanderlust Designers, updated September 14, 2026). It is a secondary source, and it gives no peso amounts. I could not open the official portal, so confirm the price on pasesparques.cl before you book.
  • How to buy. Tickets are sold online and in advance through pasesparques.cl (El Mostrador, June 10, 2026). An older page of the park's tour operators says tickets cannot be bought at the gates, dates cannot be changed after purchase, and a refund is given only if CONAF closes the whole park (torresdelpaine.com, for visits until April 30, 2026). The guide above says the same about refunds under the new system.
  • Rules. A travel operator's summary of the park regulations, last edited December 15, 2025, says you may camp only in authorized areas, that fires and stoves are banned outside marked areas because of wildfire risk, and that swimming in rivers, lagoons and lakes is prohibited because of drowning risk (Vertice, park regulations).
  • Winter guide rule. Anyone doing winter tourist activities on the W circuit or the Base Torres trail must have a guide authorized by CONAF, a rule in force from April 27, 2026 (CNN Chile, above). I did not find out what applies in spring and summer.
  • The fire of 2011. Many of the bushes and trunks in my photos are bare and grey. In December 2011, a large fire burned about 11,000 hectares, nearly 4% of the park, and about 700 people, mostly tourists, were evacuated (Al Jazeera, December 31, 2011). I do not know if the bare trees I photographed come from that fire.

What I could not check

The park's own site would not open for me, so I could not check the official price, the trail rules or the camping and refuge prices at the source. I also do not know what I paid for the ticket, the shuttle, the bus or the hostel in 2017, or the name of the campsite where I slept.

Is it worth it?

The landscapes are like nothing I have seen anywhere else, and I am glad I went. But if you come mainly for the views, a day trip from Puerto Natales with a guide, or a short walk, may be enough. If you love long walks and camping, you will probably like it more than I did. Whatever you choose, do not hike alone, and do not underestimate the weather.
