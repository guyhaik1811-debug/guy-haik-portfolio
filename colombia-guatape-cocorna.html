Guatapé and Cocorná, December 8 to 10, 2017

December 2017Guy Haik

After Comuna 13 on December 8 I went to Guatapé, a lake town east of Medellín. I spent three days there and around it: a night in a hostel, a day on a rented scooter to Cocorná for a tandem paraglider flight, and a boat tour on the reservoir. I was alone, except on the boat, which had about ten other people.

My conclusion up front: Guatapé works for a few days, but the distances are long. The hostel I liked best, Happy Buddha, is up a road that you only reach by jeep, and that is why I could not go back to it for my second night. I do not remember how I got to Guatapé, or what I paid for the boat, the scooter or the flight.

The times are my phone's clock, and Google Photos stores them in Colombian time (GMT-5). The boxes hold research I did in November 2026, each with a source and a date, so you can tell it apart from what I remember.

December 8: from a height

My first photos in Guatapé are from 16:23 to 16:57, all taken from a height over the reservoir. The viewpoint, the stone steps and a statue of the Virgin with a sign that forbids climbing on her pedestal fit the top of La Piedra del Peñol, the granite rock above the lake. I did not write it down, and I have no photo of the climb or the ticket.

Getting to Guatapé and the Rock (checked November 2026)

What I found: a guide published in May 2026 and updated on 17 November 2026 gives a bus from Medellín's Terminal del Norte at about 15,000 pesos each way and about 2 hours, with the first bus around 05:30 and the last one back around 19:00. The same guide gives the Rock's entrance fee as 35,000 pesos for foreigners and 25,000 for Colombians, with 740 steps (colombiamove.com). An article from July 2024 says 25,000 pesos per person and more than 700 steps (semana.com).

What I did not find: the opening hours, or an official page that settles the two fees. Ask at the ticket booth.

The night at Happy Buddha

At 21:58 I took one photo in the hostel, of a giant Jenga tower made of painted blocks next to a wall with the hostel's sign on it. I am almost sure it was Happy Buddha, and the sign fits. I stayed one night and enjoyed it a lot. I blurred the face of a man sitting at the table.

The hostel is up a road where you need a jeep. When I rode out the next day I had no way to get back to it, so I slept the second night somewhere else. I do not remember where.

Happy Buddha Hostel (checked November 2026)

What I found: listings of a "Happy Buddha Hostel Guatapé" on Expedia and Hotels.com.

What I did not find: a current price, how to get there, or confirmation of the jeep. I took the jeep from my own memory. Ask the hostel how to reach it before you book.

December 9: Cocorná by scooter

On December 9 I rented a scooter and rode a long way from Guatapé to Cocorná, to fly a tandem paraglider. On the way back a downpour caught me. I do not remember where I rented the scooter or what I paid, and I do not remember the name of the paragliding company or what the flight cost.

The photos are all from about 17:01 to 17:05. At 17:01 I am on a green hillside in the fog before take-off. Half a minute later my feet are hanging in the air. The town in the valley below is Cocorná. I took a short selfie video with the instructor, and I am leaving it out because he did not agree to appear on a website.

Paragliding in Cocorná (checked November 2026)

What I found: a booking page lists a tandem flight with an English-speaking instructor from US$80.22. It says about 15 to 20 minutes in the air, a minimum age of 12, free cancellation up to 24 hours before, and a meeting point called "Parapente Cocorná" (civitatis.com).

What I did not find: a price in pesos, whether the figure is per person, or which company I flew with. The page has no update date, so treat the price as a rough guide.

December 10: the reservoir by boat

On December 10 I walked down to the pier without a plan and boarded a boat with about ten other people. I do not remember the company or what I paid. First, at 12:07, a photo of the lake from shore, with boats at the pier. At 13:51 and 13:53 I was in the town's church: an interior of carved wood with gold, with a Christmas wreath and garlands, and a white and red façade with two towers, a clock and a star made of lights.

The boat photos run from 14:16 to 14:53. At 14:31 there is a long shoreline of green lawn with a ruined house in the distance, and at 14:33 the house up close. It is a burned-out two-storey building with a roof gone green, with yellow tape across the ground floor. The guide on the boat presented it as Pablo Escobar's house. I did not check that then, and I have no other source from that day.

The last photo of the day is at 16:55, back at the pier: pedal boats, an inflatable slide and a red-roofed floating restaurant on the water.

Boat tours on the Guatapé reservoir (checked November 2026)

What I found: a guide updated on 17 November 2026 puts shared boat tours at 20,000 to 35,000 pesos per person, for a 45 to 90 minute loop (colombiamove.com). One operator lists a two-hour tour with a stop at the cross of the old, submerged town of Peñol, a pass by the La Manuela farm that it describes as Pablo Escobar's, and a photo stop at the Rock. It does not show a price (tur.com).

What I did not find: which company I used, or what I paid. The first guide does not mention La Manuela.

The days after

I have no photos after 16:55 on December 10. I think I went on from here to Salento, which is the next chapter, by bus back to Medellín first. I am not sure of that, and I do not remember when I left Guatapé or what the bus cost.

What I do not remember

I do not remember how I reached Guatapé, where I slept on the night of December 9, who the group on the boat was, or what the boat, the scooter, the flight and the hostel cost. I took no notes. The photos give the three days in order, and nothing more.
