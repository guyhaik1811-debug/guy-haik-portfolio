South America · Colombia · Palomino

Palomino, November 2017

November 2017Guy Haik

Palomino is a small village on the Caribbean coast, on the edge of La Guajira, where a river reaches the sea and long beaches run on both sides. I left Minca on the morning of November 28, 2017, and by 12:45 I was already on the river. My photos cover that one day: the river, a walk along the beach, and fishermen at sunset.

My conclusion up front: Palomino is a place to slow down, and the sea there is not for casual swimming. I walked along empty beaches for hours and watched the waves from the sand, and my research for this page found that the sea here has killed tourists. I do not remember where I slept, what I paid for the river or for the room, or the name of any place I stopped at. I would rather say so than guess.

The times are my phone's clock, and Google Photos stores them in Colombian time (GMT-5). The boxes hold research I did in November 2026, each with a source and a date, so you can tell it apart from what I remember.

Getting there

I left Minca with the owner of my hostel, in his car, and I do not remember how I got from there to Palomino. The first photo of the day is from 12:45, so I was there before midday. The usual way is a minivan to Santa Marta and a bus on to Palomino, which I describe in the box below, but I cannot say that is how I went.

The road from Santa Marta (checked November 2026)

What I found: a guide updated in December 2025 gives the public-transport route as a bus from the Sierra Mar office in Santa Marta to Palomino for about 12,000 pesos and about two hours, which leaves you near the D1 supermarket on Palomino's main road (Travelers and Dreamers, updated December 24, 2025).

The main road crosses the Mendihuaca River, and by February 3, 2026 the bridge over it had collapsed during a period of heavy rain, which cut the road between Santa Marta and Palomino (Infobae, February 3, 2026). A new bridge was inaugurated on March 31, 2026, built by the Army's engineers in 12 days, and the road reopened that day (El Universal, March 31, 2026).

What I did not find: the fare in 2017, bus times, and anything newer than March on the state of the bridge. The article says so itself, so check the road before you travel.

The river, 12:45 to 13:20

In three photos from midday I am on a wide, slow river with brown water and green banks. In the second, my feet float in the water at the bottom of the frame, with a forested hill beyond the far bank. I was floating down the river on a tube. I do not remember which river it was, and I did not write down the operator, the start, the length or the price. The map in Google Photos puts the first photo near the village of Palomino, close to the river that runs through it, so I take it to be the Río Palomino, but I am not certain. The last photo shows the river narrowing between two banks, and past them the line of the sea.

River tubing near Palomino today (checked November 2026)

What I found: a Blu Radio article from March 15, 2025 says the float on the Río Palomino takes about one to two hours depending on the flow. It starts at a higher point on the river, reached by motorbike or truck from the village and followed by a short walk through the forest, and it ends where the river meets the Caribbean on the beach. It gives a price of 40,000 to 70,000 pesos a person, depending on the operator and the transport to the start (Blu Radio, March 15, 2025).

What I did not find: what the same trip cost in 2017, which river and operator I used, and whether the water level is safe today. After heavy rain a river like this can run fast, so ask at the village before you go.

A walk along the beach, 14:49 to 15:55

In the afternoon I walked along the shore, away from the village. The photos from 14:49 to 15:55 show a coast with no buildings: a line of coconut palms, a dark band of sand, a lot of driftwood and branches that the sea or the river had thrown up, and a headland ahead. At 14:49 there is a pool among the palms where a stream reaches the beach. At 15:22 there are a few yellow fishing boats pulled up, with nets hung out to dry between the trees. I do not know which stretch of beach this was or how far I walked.

The evening: lagoon and fishermen, 16:43 to 17:03

At 16:43 I was back by the mouth of a stream, where the water is held behind a bank of sand and driftwood. Two white egrets stand in the shallows, and the sea is on the left. A few minutes later, between 16:50 and 16:51, I took photos of local fishermen with a net at the river mouth. In one of them, a man in a white shirt with a red number holds a net as he wades in the shallows, with a boy beside him and a dark storm cloud over the hills. A tourist in red shorts stands on the sand next to them. I blurred the faces of the tourist and the boy.

Swimming in the sea (checked November 2026)

What I found: on January 1, 2023 a German tourist died at the beaches of Palomino. Police said she probably drowned after entering the sea outside the hours set by the municipality of Dibulla, which allows swimming from 6:00 to 17:00. Residents and tourists said the beach needs more lifeguards (Noticias Caracol, January 2, 2023).

The same Infobae piece from February 2026 shows what a red alert on the coast looks like: tourists were still in the water at Santa Marta and the police had to pull them out. The authorities' request was not to enter the sea during these episodes (Infobae, February 3, 2026).

What I did not find: whether the 6:00 to 17:00 rule is still in force in 2026, how many lifeguards the beach has now, or an official map of the dangerous stretches. My last photo of the sea is at 17:03, which is also when that rule would end.

What I do not remember

I do not remember where I slept, how many nights I stayed, what I paid for the river and the room, who the operator was, or how I left on the 29th. Google Photos has nothing at all from the 29th, so I do not know where I went next. The photos give one day, in order, and nothing more.
