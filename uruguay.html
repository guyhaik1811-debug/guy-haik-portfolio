Uruguay: Montevideo and the Rocha coast, January–February 2018

January 30 – February 7, 2018Guy Haik

This chapter covers nine days: the ferry from Buenos Aires on January 30, a stop in Montevideo, and then the Atlantic coast of the Rocha department, from Punta del Diablo in the north to La Paloma in the south, and the flight out on February 7. I have about 90 photos and three videos from these days, and I use 32 photos and two videos. I left out the ones that repeat the same view, the ones with strangers close to the camera, and a photo of a SIM card package that shows a phone number. Brazil continues in the next chapters.

My conclusion up front: Uruguay was a break for me after Brazil. I had travelled in Brazil at the peak of the season, and it was very crowded. On this coast I walked on wide empty beaches, I saw sea lions from close up, and I saw a sunset that I still remember as the most beautiful one I have seen. The two practical things to know are that my Mastercard credit card could not give me cash in Uruguay in 2018, so bring cash, and that you need to plan the buses, because there is no road into Cabo Polonio.

A note on times: the times in this chapter are the times in the file names of my photos, which is the clock of my phone. From February 3 to 7 Google Photos shows the same photos one hour later, and on January 30 it showed no difference. I write the phone clock and I say so. I do not know exactly when the phone changed its setting, so I do not give clock times as exact.

What to know today, in short (checked November 2026)
  • The ferry from Buenos Aires: 2 hours 30 minutes to 4 hours 45 minutes depending on the company, at $99 to $507 depending on the company, the season and whether you take a car. I could not tell from my photo which company I used.
  • Cabo Polonio: the department's tourism site (updated April 2026) lists the round trip from the park gate to the village at UYU 470 per person. Private vehicles cannot enter the village. Trucks cover the seven kilometers in about 30 minutes, and you can also walk, which takes more than two hours.
  • Buses: Punta del Diablo to Montevideo takes about 5 hours, and Rocha to Carrasco airport about 2 hours 35 minutes. The fares come from third-party estimates, so check at the terminal.
  • Cash and cards: most services take cards, but markets, taxis and small businesses may need cash. ATM operators can add their own surcharge. Non-resident tourists with a foreign ID pay no VAT at hotels and other lodging, according to the Ministry of Tourism.
  • La Paloma and Laguna de Rocha: I am not sure whether my photos of the breakwater and the sunset are from Costa Azul, from the port or from the lagoon bar, so I name none of them.

The sources and the details are in the boxes below the story. These things change, so check before you go.

January 30: the ferry and Montevideo

I slept in Buenos Aires on the night of January 29, after the flight from Iguazú. The hostel was a Milhouse Hostel, but I do not remember which of their locations in the city. On January 30 I took a ferry to Montevideo. I do not remember which company, from which terminal, what the ticket cost, or when we sailed. My photo of the ship is from 11:05 by the phone clock. The hull shows the word "ATLANTIC" with the next letter cut off by the frame, and I do not know what the rest of the name is.

At 13:15 I photographed the skyline of Montevideo from a road along the water, with a guard rail in the front. The tall blue-glass tower on the right is the one that stands out in the skyline. I do not know the name of the road.

The ferry from Buenos Aires to Montevideo (checked November 2026)

What I found: The comparison site Direct Ferries says that the crossing takes between 2 hours 30 minutes and 4 hours 45 minutes depending on the company, and that a ticket costs between $99 and $507 depending on the company, the season and whether you travel with a car. The same route page lists Buquebus sailings of 2 hours 45 minutes and 4 hours 15 minutes, and the page for the opposite direction also lists Colonia Express with 4 hours 45 minutes.

What I did not find: the company, the price and the schedule of January 30, 2018, and whether my ferry was a direct one or went through Colonia. I cannot tell from my photo which company it was.

I bought a local SIM card in Montevideo that afternoon, at 15:38 by the phone clock. I do not show the package, because it carries a phone number. I do not remember what I paid, and I do not remember where I slept that night.

January 31: Punta del Diablo

On January 31 I was in Punta del Diablo, a fishing village on the coast of the Rocha department. I do not remember how I got there from Montevideo. It is a bus ride of about five hours, so I think I went by bus, and that I left in the morning, but I have no ticket or photo of it. My first photo is at 12:47 by the phone clock. I think I slept in a hostel, and I do not remember its name.

The bus from Montevideo to the Rocha coast (checked November 2026)

What I found: The blog The Daily Packers says that the bus between Punta del Diablo and Montevideo takes around 5 hours and ends at Tres Cruces, the main terminal of Montevideo, and that two companies run it, Rutas del Sol and COT. The route page of a route planner estimates the ticket to Tres Cruces at $20–27 and the trip at about 4 hours 10 minutes.

What I did not find: the schedule and the fare of January 2018, and whether I took one of these buses. These are third-party sites, not the companies, and the fares are estimates.

Cash and cards in Uruguay (checked November 2026)

What I found: The money blog Sin Comisiones (July 2026) says that the main ATM networks in Uruguay belong to Banco República (BROU) and Banred, that the ATM operator can add its own surcharge, which depends on the network, and that you can use a card in most services but may need cash in markets, taxis and small businesses. The Ministry of Tourism says that non-resident tourists who show an ID issued abroad get zero VAT in hotels and other lodging, so the tax is not charged and there is no refund later.

What I did not find: whether a credit card from my bank gave cash at an ATM in 2018, which fees applied then, and any source that describes the practice I used at a hostel in Punta del Diablo. I do not give advice about how to get cash.

My Mastercard credit card could not withdraw cash in Uruguay in 2018. I do not know why, and I did not look into it then. In the hostel in Punta del Diablo I asked the owner to charge my card for a larger amount than the price of the stay, for example 1,000, and to give me the difference, for example 900, in cash. I am not saying that this is a good method or that it is allowed. It is what I did, and I do not remember the exact amounts, which hostel it was, or what it cost me in fees. I did not find any source that describes this practice.

February 1 and 2: Barra de Valizas

From Punta del Diablo I went to Barra de Valizas, a small village at the mouth of a river, with sand streets. I do not remember how I got there, or where I slept on the nights of February 1 and 2. My first photo of the village is a sunset at 19:35 on February 1, with the roofs of the village in the dark at the bottom.

On February 2 I joined a kind of organized trip, and I do not remember who ran it or what it cost. My first photos are at 13:46 by the phone clock, from the top of a big dune. You see the sea at the right, a wide beach, the river mouth, and the village behind a lagoon. I made a video of the same place.

Later in the day I took a boat trip on the water. I think it was on the river, and not in a lagoon or at sea, but I am not sure. The photos show a calm, dark blue river with green banks, and a small wooden dock with a boat with a green canopy, and a white bird that stands on the roof of the boat, next to a hut among tall trees.

The trip also included a very big tree with a hollow trunk and roots that spread over the ground, and I stood inside the hollow. At night there was a kind of festival, with a crowd and fires. I made a video, but it has faces of people I do not know, so I do not show it, and I do not remember what the festival was.

February 3: Cabo Polonio

On February 3 I went to Cabo Polonio, by bus and alone. I think I got to the village on the truck that goes through the dunes, but I am not sure. I did not stay long, and I do not remember where I slept that night, if I slept there at all. My photos are from 14:04 to 15:25 by the phone clock. Cabo Polonio has no road, and it is a national park with a lighthouse, a small village and a colony of sea lions.

The sea lions were on the rocks at the edge of the water, and they were close to me, not far away. They are dark, and from a distance they look like part of the rock. I like nature and animals, so seeing them that close mattered to me.

Getting into Cabo Polonio (checked November 2026)

What I found: The tourism site of the Rocha department (schedules and costs, updated April 2026) lists the round trip from the park gate to the village at UYU 470 per person, children under 5 free, and daily entry times from 05:30 to 20:00 and exit times from 06:00 to 18:00. It lists no separate park entrance fee. A newspaper article from January 2026, Conclusión, says that private vehicles cannot enter the village, that open safari-style trucks cover the seven kilometers in 30 minutes, and that you can also arrive on foot or on a horse. An Infocasas blog says that the entrance is at kilometer 264.500 of Route 10, and that the walk takes more than two hours.

What I did not find: what I paid in 2018, whether I came by truck or in another way, and the schedule of 2018. I also did not find which company runs the gate, and the page does not say.

February 4: La Pedrera and La Paloma

On February 4 I went south along the coast. Google Photos places some of my photos from this day in La Pedrera, some in Punta Rubia y Santa Isabel, and some in La Paloma. I took 27 photos between 14:46 and 18:12 by the phone clock, and I did not match each photo to a place, so I describe what I see. I think I went from La Pedrera to La Paloma by bus, alone, but I do not remember the details.

I slept on the nights of February 4 and 5 in a hostel, and I do not remember its name. On the evening of February 4 I have photos of the sun low behind thin clouds, over the sea and over a beach at the edge of the town.

February 5: La Paloma, Costa Azul and the sunset

February 5 was a long day in La Paloma. At 10:39 I photographed the sign of the municipality, with a drawing of a lighthouse and gulls, and at 11:16 I photographed a paper map of the town, which I held in my hand. The map is titled "La Paloma – La Aguada – Costa Azul".

The first photo that Google Photos places in Costa Azul is from 12:29 by the phone clock, and it shows a stone breakwater made of big concrete blocks with a green steel tower at its end. I took a selfie under the tower at 12:34 by the file name of the photo. At 13:51 I photographed the white lighthouse at the end of a road. The tourism site of the department names it the Cabo Santa María lighthouse in La Paloma. I did not check my photo against a sign, so I give the name from this source.

La Paloma and Costa Azul (checked November 2026)

What I found: The newspaper Minuto Uno says that Costa Azul is part of La Paloma, a few kilometers from its center, and that from Montevideo it is 240 kilometers to La Paloma and then four more kilometers along the coastal road. The same article lists a visit to the Cabo Santa María lighthouse among the things to do there.

What I did not find: how I got from La Paloma to Costa Azul (I think I took a bus, alone, but I am not sure), the price, and whether the breakwater in my photos is in Costa Azul or in the port of La Paloma. Google Photos gives only the first photo to Costa Azul.

The lagoon, the kites and the sunset

In the late afternoon, from 18:59 to 19:09 by the phone clock, I photographed kitesurfers on a wide lagoon, with a low green shore, fishing nets and small boats on the edge of the water. I think this was the lagoon on the edge of La Paloma, but Google Photos places these photos only in the Rocha department, so I do not give a name. At 19:01 I photographed a poster on a wall, "Recursos de la Pesca Artesanal en la Costa Atlántica Uruguaya", from the national office of aquatic resources. It lists the fish and the shellfish of the coast, with photos, and I do not translate the list.

The hostel took us to see the sunset in a 4x4. The most beautiful sunset I have ever seen was on this trip, as I remember it, and I think it is this evening, but I am not sure that it was not February 4. We got out and walked a path of beaten earth for a few minutes with the group, and the sun went down behind the grass. A sign at the side of the path says "Sendero Barra de la Laguna", with the name of the national system of protected areas of Uruguay. The Rocha tourism site lists a trail with the same name in the protected landscape of the Laguna de Rocha. So I think that this was the place, but Google Photos places my photos only in the Rocha department.

Laguna de Rocha (checked November 2026)

What I found: The Ministry of the Environment says that the lagoon opens and closes to the Atlantic through a sandy bar, and that a community of traditional fishermen has lived near the bar for 70 years, living on shrimp, blue crab and fish such as flounder, croaker and silverside. The Infocasas blog says that the lagoon is about 8 kilometers from La Paloma, and that it has around 200 species of birds.

What I did not find: whether the place of my sunset photos is the bar of the lagoon, the exact route of the 4x4, and the price of the ride. I do not remember if I paid for it separately.

February 6 and 7: Rocha and the airport

On February 6 I have two photos, from 12:19 by the phone clock, which Google Photos places in the city of Rocha. They show a paved plaza next to a modern glass building with a taxi stand sign, and I do not remember what I did there. I think I went from La Paloma by bus, but I do not remember it.

On February 7 I flew from Carrasco airport, near Montevideo, to Brazil. I think I went to the airport by bus from Rocha, but I am not sure. My only photo of that day is of the airport building at 12:23 by the phone clock, from the road: a white curved roof over a glass hall. I do not know the time of the flight, the airline, or the route, and I have no ticket. The next photos in my library are from Florianópolis on February 8, so I assume that I arrived there on the evening of the 7th or the morning of the 8th, but I do not know.

From the Rocha coast to Carrasco airport (checked November 2026)

What I found: The route planner Rome2rio says that COT runs a bus from Rocha to Carrasco airport every four hours, for $13–20, and that the trip takes about 2 hours 35 minutes. The page for Montevideo and Rocha says that the bus from the Tres Cruces terminal to Rocha takes about 3 hours and costs $13–22.

What I did not find: the schedule of February 7, 2018, the fare then, and the company that I used. These are estimates from a third-party site.
