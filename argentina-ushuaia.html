South America · Argentina · Ushuaia

Ushuaia, October 2017

October 18 to 22, 2017Guy Haik

Ushuaia is the southernmost city I reached on this trip, at the bottom of Argentina on the island of Tierra del Fuego. I spent five days there, and most of what I did was outdoors: a national park, a lagoon under a glacier, and a boat trip on the Beagle Channel. This chapter is in the order it happened. Boxes like the one below hold research I did in November 2026, with the source and the date, so you can tell it apart from what I remember.

Before you go: what I checked in November 2026

Tierra del Fuego National Park: the national parks tariff table, valid from June 1, 2026, lists a daily pass for this park in the "General" category at ARS 40,000 (Argentina.gob.ar). The table does not say that "General" means non-resident foreigners, so I cannot give you the exact foreign rate. Infobae reports the same figure, and a 50% discount for a second day within 72 hours that can only be bought online (Infobae, May 15, 2026).

Laguna Esmeralda: a 9 km round trip, about 4 to 5 hours, and about 20 minutes from town on Route 3. The best season is October to March, and snow and ice can stay into October. The author recommends a guided trip in winter and warns that there is no phone signal at the trailhead. The article does not mention an entrance fee, so ask locally (Sol Salute, March 9, 2026).

Getting here: Lima, Buenos Aires, Ushuaia

I flew from Lima to Buenos Aires and on to Ushuaia, and my first photos in town are from the afternoon of Wednesday, October 18. The connection almost went wrong. I did not know that Buenos Aires has more than one airport. At the first airport, an information desk told me I had to go to the other one. Taxi drivers at the entrance offered the transfer at an inflated price, so I walked away from the airport, stopped a taxi on the road, and asked in Spanish to be taken fast to the other airport because I was about to miss my flight to Ushuaia. I gave the driver all the money I had left from Peru. He was kind about it, and I made the flight by a miracle. I did not write down which airport was which.

The two Buenos Aires airports (checked November 2026)

Ezeiza (EZE) and Aeroparque (AEP) are about 32 km apart. A private transfer takes 40 to 60 minutes in normal traffic, and taxis and ride apps can take much longer at peak hours. Public transport takes over two hours and needs at least one change, and a bus line (line 8) runs between the two airports. Shared shuttles with fixed schedules also exist, and the article names two companies, Tienda León and Minibus Ezeiza. It gives no prices and no minimum connection time between the two airports (Canal 26, March 1, 2026).

I did not check which airports the airlines use for Lima flights today. Whatever your route, check before you book which airport each flight uses, and if they differ, plan for a transfer of an hour or more.

Where I stayed

I spent the first night in a hostel near the airport. Later I moved to Patagonia Pais, a hostel in Ushuaia. I am almost sure about the name, so treat it as probable. It appears in hostel booking listings (Dorms.com), but I could not confirm that it is still open today. Before I left we had a Friday dinner at the hostel.

The people

For me, the magic moments of a trip are meeting good people, and in Ushuaia I spent most of these days with a group of travellers I had met there. I am not naming anyone, and I blurred their faces. Their photos are in this chapter because they are part of how I remember it.

October 19: Tierra del Fuego National Park

Google Photos places my photos from this day in Tierra del Fuego National Park, between 13:04 and 15:04 by my phone's clock. I do not remember how we got there. The park is west of town, and the water in these photos is the Beagle Channel.

Some of the group's photos were taken on another traveller's camera, and its clock was wrong, so I do not give times for them. They show a pebble beach, a forest trail and a wooden pier with a small post office building. I believe they are from the same day in the park, but I cannot prove it.

The post office at the end of the world (checked November 2026)

The building looks like the "Postal del Fin del Mundo", a small tin house in Ensenada Bay inside the park. It is described as the southernmost post office in South America, was inaugurated on December 12, 1997, and you can mail a postcard from there for a small fee. Atlas Obscura advises you to have the exact address and postal code with you (Atlas Obscura, undated). I cannot tell you if it is open today.

October 20: Laguna Esmeralda

The next morning we walked to Laguna Esmeralda. My photos at the lagoon are from 12:39 to 12:55. There was snow on the ground, and the lagoon sat in a valley with snow on both sides. A sign at the lagoon, numbered 8, marked the photo point, and two dogs were waiting beside it. I do not remember how we got to the trailhead.

October 20: the Beagle Channel by boat

That afternoon I went out on the Beagle Channel on a boat trip. I do not remember the company, the price, or how I booked it. The photos run from 15:52 to 17:14 by my phone's clock, and Google Photos labels the lighthouse photos "Beagle Channel". The stops look like the route that the tours listed below describe: a rock covered with cormorants, a rock with sea lions, and a red and white lighthouse. I am not able to name the islands from my own memory.

Boat trips on the Beagle Channel (checked November 2026)

One Expedia listing, with no date on the page, shows a four-hour trip at US$142 per adult, with departures at 9:00 and 14:00 marked as pending confirmation. Its route lists the Les Éclaireurs lighthouse, Isla de los Lobos for sea lions, and Isla de los Pájaros for Magellanic and Imperial cormorants (Expedia). This is a reseller, so compare the price and the departure times with the operators before you book.

The Les Éclaireurs lighthouse is a brick tower 11 metres tall, with its light 22.5 metres above sea level, on one of the Les Éclaireurs islands about 5 nautical miles east of Ushuaia. It entered service on December 23, 1920 (Wikipedia). I believe that is the lighthouse in my photos, but Google Photos only says "Beagle Channel".

October 20, evening: the welcome sign

At 18:16 the group stopped for photos at the "Ushuaia, fin del mundo" welcome sign on the waterfront, which was after the boat trip. The photo below was taken on another traveller's phone. The sign says "Los Pobladores de Ushuaia les damos la bienvenida", which means the settlers of Ushuaia welcome you. The faces of the others are blurred.

October 21 and 22: in town

On Saturday, October 21, I walked around Ushuaia. The photos are from 14:53 to 18:40. This is what I saw, and I do not remember the name of the café or of the restaurant with the crab tank.

Google Photos places the coffee photo in the centre of town, near Avenida Maipú, but it only gives the street area, not the café. My last photo of Ushuaia was taken at 00:13 on Sunday, October 22, 2017, in the Hard Rock Cafe Ushuaia. The stage was empty, and I do not remember what I did there.

October 22: leaving Ushuaia

I left Ushuaia on Sunday, October 22, 2017, a few hours after the Hard Rock Cafe. To get out I had to go on board a huge ferry, which felt to me like a giant raft carrying vehicles across the water. It is an important detail if you plan to leave Ushuaia overland: the way out is not a simple road, and it includes a water crossing. My photos of the ferry ramp and of the empty steppe on the way are dated 13:35 to 17:13 that day, and Google Photos places the later ones in Chile, so they belong to the next chapter and not to this one. I do not remember the name of the ferry, what it cost, or how long we waited.
